pyDENSSmulti: 3D maps from several contrasts

pyDENSSmulti reconstructs a low-resolution 3D map of a particle from several SAS curves of the same particle in different contrasts: a SANS contrast-variation series (H2O/D2O mixtures, deuterated components) and/or SAXS. One curve leaves the internal structure of a DENSS map largely undetermined (Konarev & Svergun, Nature Methods 18, 244 (2021); Grant’s reply, ibid. 246); several contrasts add exactly that information.

  • launcher tab pyDENSSmulti, or stand-alone: pysasfit-densitymulti [data files]

  • scripts: from pysasfit import densitymulti as dm

  • no external program: the iteration is pySASfit’s own numpy/scipy code (DENSS need not be installed)

Method

The iteration follows DENSS (T. D. Grant, Nature Methods 15, 191 (2018)), re-implemented from the paper: Fourier transform of the map, replacement of the shell-averaged intensity by the measured one (phases kept), back transform, real-space constraints, shrink-wrap support (Gaussian smoothing from 3 to 1.5 voxels, threshold 0.2 of the maximum, largest connected region from step 500, re-centring up to step 1000). The data enter through a regularised p(r) fit up to Dmax (no positivity: a contrast whose components have opposite signs has a p(r) with negative parts), evaluated on the q shells of the grid.

Two schemes combine the contrasts.

Component basis (default)

The particle consists of M components with known SLDs ρ_c in every contrast. The unknowns are volume fractions φ_c(x) ∈ [0, 1] with Σ_c φ_c ≤ 1 (the rest is solvent). Contrast k sees

Δρ_k(x) = Σ_c C[k, c] φ_c(x), C[k, c] = ρ_c(f_k) − ρ_s(f_k)

with the contrast matrix C (f_k: D2O fraction of contrast k). In every step each contrast makes its own Fourier step and proposes a contrast map Δρ’_k(x); the new φ(x) is, in every voxel, the weighted bounded least-squares solution of Δρ’_k ≈ Σ_c C[k, c] φ_c (weights per contrast; accelerated projected gradient onto φ ≥ 0, Σφ ≤ 1).

  • Match points help. A contrast in which component c is matched has a zero in column c: it says nothing about c (so it cannot pull c towards the solvent, as an equal-weight average of the contrasts would) and everything about the others.

  • H/D exchange is part of ρ_c(f): ρ_c(f) = ρ_c,H2O + f · x · (ρ_c,D2O − ρ_c,H2O), with ρ_c,D2O the SLD in D2O at full exchange of the labile H and x the exchanged fraction.

  • SAXS curves use the X-ray SLD of every component and of water.

  • Volumes: Σ φ_c × voxel volume is the volume of every component.

The Contrasts panel shows C, its rank and condition number, the sensitivity of every component (1/√((CᵀWC)⁻¹)_cc) and warnings: rank deficiency, a component matched in every contrast, or determined by one curve only, poor conditioning.

Averaging (DENSS-Multiple type)

After J. Sumner & S. Qian, BBA Advances 2, 100063 (2022) (whose code is not public): one SLD map ρ(x), the same in every contrast. Each contrast makes its Fourier step on its own contrast map; the SLD is the weighted real-space average of the contrasts’ proposals ρ_s,k + Δρ’_k / b. pySASfit adds an occupancy b(x) ∈ [0, 1] (Δρ_k = b (ρ − ρ_s,k)), fitted per voxel: without it the solvent region turns into a fictitious material with the mean solvent SLD and the particle is not localised. With the occupancy, the average is also the least-squares SLD. It assumes no H/D exchange and no matched components; it is kept for comparison. Optional bounds of the SLD (Reconstruction panel).

Scale

  • fitted (default): one factor for all curves (relative concentrations per contrast in the table). The amplitude is fixed by taking the densest voxels as fully occupied (99.5th percentile of Σφ = 1).

  • absolute: data in 1/cm; I(q) = n_p |A(q)|² with the particle number density n_p from the concentration (mg/ml) and the molar mass (kDa). Then the volume fractions and volumes are absolute.

A flat background (incoherent scattering) is fitted by no part of the iteration: subtract it (column bg, or “Fit tail backgrounds”: I = A q⁻⁴ + bg over the last 30 % of every curve).

Several maps

With maps > 1, independent maps (consecutive seeds) are computed in threads, aligned onto the map with the lowest χ² by their principal axes (8 sign choices, mirror images included: SAS cannot tell enantiomers apart), and averaged; the spread of every φ_c is kept. Each map is also saved in map_xx.

Panels

panel

content

Data

loaded curves; Add selected as contrasts (D2O fraction guessed from the name: “42% D2O”, “D2O_100”, “H2O”)

Components

name, SLD in H2O, SLD in D2O (full exchange), exchanged fraction, X-ray SLD, match point; presets (typical values only); solvent SLDs of H2O and D2O

Contrasts

per curve: radiation, D2O %, solvent SLD (override), relative concentration, weight, bg, q range; contrast matrix and identifiability

Reconstruction

scheme, Dmax (with a 3.5 Rg estimate), grid points (default: from the largest q, at least 32), oversampling (3), steps, shrink-wrap threshold, seed, scale, SLD range (averaging), maps and threads; Run / Stop / Self test

Density (3D)

isosurface of every φ_c at a level (0.5), in the colours of the contrast matrix; the larger components see-through

I(q)

every contrast with its map curve (exact orientational average of the voxel map, Debye sum over the lattice), residuals

Convergence

χ² per contrast (on the shells) and support volume per step

Slices

three central slices of a φ_c, of its spread (averages), of the SLD map or of a contrast map Δρ_k

File → Example loads synthetic curves (core-shell sphere or two touching spheres, a perdeuterated and a protonated protein, 0/42/70/100 % D2O, 1 % noise) with their components, to try the tool.

Every run writes into its own folder (Preferences → pyDENSSmulti): multi_phi_<c>_<name>.mrc (φ_c), multi_drho_<k>_<curve>.mrc (Δρ_k), multi_support.mrc, multi_sld.mrc (averaging), multi_fit_<k>.dat (q, I, err, I_map), multi_stats_by_step.dat, multi_summary.json. File → Open a result folder reads it back; the last session reopens the runs.

Checks (synthetic data, 1 % noise, 32³ grid)

  • One contrast, sphere R = 2 nm (self test): Rg 15.76 Å (exact 15.49 Å, +1.7 %). DENSS 1.8.7 on the same curve: 16.10 Å (+3.9 %). χ² of the map (exact Debye average at the data points) 86, DENSS map 85. Half-width q shells are used (full-width shells: χ² 309).

  • Core-shell sphere (perdeuterated protein core R = 2 nm, protein shell to 3.5 nm; 0, 42, 70, 100 % D2O; the shell is matched at 42 %): absolute scale: volumes 33 900 and 146 200 Å³ (exact 33 510 and 146 080 Å³); φ_core ≈ 1 inside 15 Å, φ_shell ≈ 1 between 20 and 30 Å. Fitted scale: 30 200 and 124 700 Å³.

  • Two touching spheres of the two components: centres 32.9 Å apart (exact 35 Å), 3.6 % overlap of the two maps.

  • Averaging scheme without exchange (SLD 4 core, 1 shell): SLD ≈ 4 in the core, ≈ 1 in the shell, total volume 179 900 Å³ (exact 179 600 Å³). With exchange (the core-shell example) its single SLD is a compromise (≈ 7 in the core, ≈ 2 in the shell) and the χ² are higher – as expected.

In a script

import numpy as np
from pysasfit import densitymulti as dm
from pysasfit.toolbox.merge import read_curves

components = [dm.Component("protein", 1.82, 3.13, exchange=0.9, sld_xray=12.3),
              dm.Component("d-protein", 6.9, 8.2, exchange=0.9, sld_xray=12.3)]
contrasts = []
for fn, f in (("cv_0.dat", 0.0), ("cv_42.dat", 0.42), ("cv_70.dat", 0.70), ("cv_100.dat", 1.0)):
    c = read_curves(fn)[0]                                          # q in 1/nm
    contrasts.append(dm.Contrast(fn, c.q, c.I, c.dI, d2o=f))
C, rs, _ = dm.contrast_matrix(components, contrasts)
print(dm.identifiability(C, names=[c.name for c in components])["messages"])
r = dm.reconstruct(components, contrasts, dm.Settings(dmax_nm=12.0, seed=1))
print(r.chi2, r.volumes)                                            # per contrast; per component (Å³)
maps = dm.reconstruct_many(components, contrasts, dm.Settings(dmax_nm=12.0), nmaps=8, cores=4)
avg = dm.align_and_average(maps)
dm.save_result(avg, "my_run")                                       # MRC maps, fits, summary

Limits

  • Low resolution, as DENSS: about Dmax / (grid points / oversampling). Larger grids (64³) take about 8× longer.

  • The component SLDs must be right: a component that is “matched” with a slightly wrong SLD still scatters a little in that contrast. The exchanged fraction can be varied to test this.

  • The alignment by principal axes fails for nearly spherical or highly symmetric particles (several orientations with the same inertia); then compare the single maps.
