pyDENSSmulti: API reference

Generated from the docstrings.

pyDENSSmulti core: 3D reconstruction from several SAS contrasts with a DENSS-type iteration (own numpy code).

The iteration follows the DENSS algorithm of T. D. Grant (Nature Methods 15, 191 (2018)): Fourier transform of the map, replacement of the shell-averaged intensity by the measured one (phases kept), back transform, real-space constraints, shrink-wrap support. It is written here from the published description; no DENSS code is used or imported.

Two schemes combine the contrasts:

"components" (default, the component basis)

The particle is made of M components with known scattering-length densities ρ_c(f_k) in every contrast k (D2O fraction f_k, partial H/D exchange, or X-rays). The unknowns are volume fractions φ_c(x) ∈ [0, 1] with Σ_c φ_c ≤ 1 (the rest is solvent). Contrast k sees Δρ_k(x) = Σ_c C[k, c] φ_c(x) with the contrast matrix C[k, c] = ρ_c(f_k) − ρ_s(f_k). After the Fourier step every contrast proposes its own Δρ’_k(x); the new φ(x) is the weighted, bounded least-squares solution of Δρ’_k(x) ≈ Σ_c C[k, c] φ_c(x) in every voxel. A contrast in which a component is matched has a zero in that column: it says nothing about that component and everything about the others, so match points help instead of pulling the matched component towards the solvent.

"average" (after DENSS-Multiple, Sumner & Qian, BBA Advances 2, 100063 (2022); written from the paper)

One SLD map ρ(x), the same in every contrast (no H/D exchange), and an occupancy b(x) ∈ [0, 1]: contrast k sees Δρ_k = b (ρ − ρ_s,k). After the Fourier step b follows from a per-voxel least-squares fit of the proposals, and ρ is DENSS-Multiple’s real-space (weighted) average of the proposals ρ_s,k + Δρ’_k / b, which is also the least-squares SLD for that occupancy. The occupancy is an addition to the paper’s scheme: without it the solvent region becomes a fictitious material with the mean solvent SLD and the particle is not localised. Kept for comparison: with H/D exchange or matched components its single SLD per voxel is a compromise.

Units: q in 1/nm on input and output (as everywhere in pySASfit), Å inside (grid, voxel, maps); SLD in 1e-6 Å⁻² (= 1e10 cm⁻²); intensities in the unit of the data. With scale="absolute" the intensity is I(q) = n_p |A(q)|² in 1/cm with the particle number density n_p from concentration (mg/ml) and molar mass (kDa).

Regularised (second-difference Tikhonov) p(r) fit up to dmax_A, without positivity (a contrast with components of opposite sign has a p(r) with negative parts). λ by the discrepancy principle (reduced χ² ≈ 1, or 1.2 × the smallest reachable χ² when the errors are underestimated).

pysasfit.densitymulti.core.make_grid(dmax_A, qmax_A, oversampling=3.0, n=None, nmax=128, shell_width=0.5)[source]

Cubic grid of side oversampling × Dmax with n points (even); n from the data qmax when None (q_max of the grid = π / voxel ≥ qmax of the data). The q shells are shell_width × 2π/side wide: half the Fourier sampling of the box (default) fits the minima of the curves markedly better than full-width shells (sphere test: χ² 86 instead of 309 with 1 % noise; Rg +1.7 % instead of +3.2 %).

pysasfit.densitymulti.core.debye_profile(d, voxel, q_A)[source]

Exact orientational average |A(q)|² of the voxel map d (Σ_voxels d e^{iqr}, voxel-volume units) at the given q (1/Å): Debye sum over the pair-distance histogram of the lattice (autocorrelation by a zero-padded FFT, binned by the integer r²), times the form factor of a sphere with the voxel volume. Used for the final fits; the iteration itself works on shells.

pysasfit.densitymulti.core.match_sld(I0, solvent, conc=None)[source]

Solvent SLD at the match point of the whole particle (1e-6 Å⁻²) from the forward intensities of several contrasts, I0_k = b c_k (ρ̄ − ρ_s,k)² (classical contrast variation: √I0 is linear in ρ_s and vanishes at the match point; with H/D exchange ρ̄ changes with the D2O fraction and the result is the solvent SLD at which it is matched). NaN if not determined (fewer than two contrasts or no change of the solvent).
