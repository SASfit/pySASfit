Theory

This part of the documentation describes the mathematics behind every step of an inversion with pysasfit.inversion: the forward model, how the integral is turned into a linear system, how that system is regularized, how the regularization strength is chosen, and how the uncertainty of the result is estimated. The last chapter records the investigations that led to the present set of methods (what was tried, what failed and why).

Notation

\(q_i,\ i=1\dots M\)

scattering vector magnitudes of the \(M\) data points

\(b_i,\ \sigma_i\)

background-subtracted intensity \(I(q_i)-I_{bg}\) and its standard uncertainty \(\Delta I(q_i)\)

\(r\), \(D_{max}\), \(r_{max}\)

real-space variable (pair distance or radius), maximum particle dimension, upper end of the \(r\) grid

\(p(r)\), \(x(r)\)

unknown function: pair-distance distribution (PDDF) or weighted size distribution \(x(R)=N(R)\,R^{\alpha}\)

\(K(q,r)\)

kernel of the Fredholm integral

\(\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}\), \(\mathbf{x}\)

discretized kernel and vector of unknowns (point values or basis coefficients)

\(\mathbf{W}=\mathrm{diag}(1/\sigma_i^2)\)

statistical weights

\(\chi^2\), \(\chi^2_r\)

\(\chi^2=\sum_i\left[(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x})_i-b_i\right]^2/\sigma_i^2\); in this package \(\chi^2_r=\chi^2/M\) (no subtraction of the number of parameters, as in SASfit’s C code)

\(\mathbf{L}\), \(\lambda\)

regularization (penalty) operator and its weight

Main references

The key papers are Glatter (1977) for the B-spline IFT, Moore (1980) for the information content, Hansen (2000) and Vestergaard & Hansen (2006) for the Bayesian IFT, Kohlbrecher & Breßler (2022) for SASfit’s EM solvers, Chae, Martin & Walker (2019) for EM with signed kernels, Silverman et al. (1990) for EM with smoothing, and Hansen’s Regularization Tools. The complete, topic-ordered list with notes on where each work is used is in References.
