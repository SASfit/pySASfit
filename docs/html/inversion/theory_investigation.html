Development history and findings

This chapter keeps the reasoning behind the present methods – including what was tried and discarded – for anyone extending the package. All numbers refer to tests/data/test.dat (bilognormal spheres with modes near 60 and 180 nm, \(q\) in nm-1, 91 log-spaced points, background \(\approx0.1\)) unless stated otherwise.

The null space of the second-difference penalty

With the \(j_0\) kernel and the plain second-difference penalty, GCV, the L-curve and the evidence all gave \(\chi^2_r\) in the hundreds (EM + smoothing 586, Tikhonov + GCV 34 600). Constants and linear ramps are not penalized, and with a kernel condition number of \(\sim10^{16}\) these free directions are enough to make every criterion choose an oscillating solution. Hansen’s (2000) boundary terms remove the null space (condition number of \(\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\) \(\sim9\times10^3\)) and gave a clean evidence maximum.

Regional overfitting at high \(q\)

A good overall \(\chi^2_r\) hid an unbalanced fit: with the Hansen penalty on the 95-point grid the high-\(q\) third of the data had \(\chi^2\approx0.02\) (the fit followed the noise), the low-\(q\) third about 0.8. Scans that each held everything else fixed ruled out the obvious causes:

  • \(\lambda\): no value gave all three regions near 1 at once.

  • \(D_{max}\): 500 nm was too small (low-\(q\) \(\chi^2\) 3.35 to 0.8 up to \(D_{max}\approx800\)), which led to the suggestion \(D_{max}=\pi/q_{min}\); the high-\(q\) value stayed at 0.02 for every \(D_{max}\).

  • number of grid points above \(\approx75\) and \(r_{min}\): no effect.

Two attempted fixes, now retired:

  • Two-segment \(\lambda\) (independent \(\lambda\) for low and high \(r\), chosen by a two-hyperparameter evidence search): within 0.3–0.6 % of the single-\(\lambda\) result, never improved the high-\(q\) fit. The \(j_0\) kernel couples every data point to the whole \(r\) range, so the noise-following component is not localized in \(r\).

  • Hann-tapered boundary (\(p=w(r)\,p_{free}\) with a raised-cosine roll-off over the last quarter of \([0,D_{max}]\)): moved the high-\(q\) \(\chi^2\) from 0.023 to 0.21, but needed a ridge stabilizer, and was superseded by the B-spline representation.

The explanation is Shannon undersampling of log-spaced data (Forward model, data and information content): at high \(q\) there are only 0.45 data points per Shannon channel, so a model with one free parameter per channel can follow every point. Reducing the number of free parameters with a B-spline basis (Discretization of the integral) brings the high-\(q\) \(\chi^2\) to about 1 for every solver and increases the normalized evidence (\(\ln Z\) from \(-153.0\) to \(-118.8\)). The retired solvers remain callable by their old keys (RETIRED_SOLVERS) but are no longer offered.

Bootstrap with forced \(\chi^2_r=1\)

The bootstrap exploded (std of \(p\) up to 400) when combined with forced \(\chi^2_r=1\), because the replicates were centred on the noisy data. Fixed by centring on the fit and by the \(\lambda\) floor (Uncertainty of the recovered distribution).

Hansen MaxEnt

The CG transcription of maxent.m jammed at the positivity bound and the registry wrapper ignored \(\sigma_i\); replaced by weighted L-BFGS-B with a Newton polish and the discrepancy principle (EM, maximum entropy and signed kernels).

Signed EM

Three problems were found and fixed on 2026-10-10: the shifted KL misfit was blind to the high-\(q\) data (fixed by \(\chi^2\) row weighting), Biggs–Andrews extrapolation could drift away from the fixed point (safeguard), and too coarse quadrature with few grid points ((5)). MaxEnt regularization with a positive/negative entropy was added as an alternative to smoothing.

Few grid points

A uniform point grid with step \(>\pi/q_{max}\) undersamples the \(j_0\) kernel (\(\chi^2_r\) 460–840 for \(N\le45\)); this affects every solver. The fine quadrature of the node and spline representations removes the limitation; with cubic interpolation 30 uniform points suffice (\(\chi^2_r\) 1.18).

Reduced solver list

Solvers that differed in a single setting were merged and given options (e.g. EM + smoothing with discrepancy principle or L-curve, Bayesian evidence with three penalties); the B-spline solvers were replaced by the generic B-spline option. Old keys are mapped by SOLVER_ALIASES.

Non-negativity is not a default

\(p(r)\ge0\) holds only for homogeneous contrast. For core–shell, multilayer or contrast-variation systems \(p(r)\) has negative parts that carry structural information. Non-negativity is therefore only used where it is part of the method (EM, MaxEnt, ARLS \(\ge0\)) and never imposed on the signed solvers.

Open questions

  • Automatic choice of the number of B-spline coefficients and of the knot spacing (e.g. by the normalized evidence).

  • Penalties scaled by the node spacing (smoothness in \(r\) rather than in the index) for non-uniform point grids.

  • The default level \(m\) of the signed MaxEnt is a heuristic.

  • Moore’s sine-series basis for the PDDF.

  • Whether the quoted \(\sigma_i\) of test.dat are well calibrated at high \(q\).
