Regularization and choice of the regularization strength

Weighted least squares

All linear methods work on the statistically weighted system

\[\tilde{\mathbf A} = \mathbf{W}^{1/2}\mathbf{A},\qquad \tilde{\mathbf b} = \mathbf{W}^{1/2}\mathbf{b},\qquad \|\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf x-\tilde{\mathbf b}\|^2 = \chi^2 ,\]

(solver_registry._weighted). Without the weights the few high-intensity low-\(q\) points dominate: on test.dat \(\sigma\) spans four decades (0.01 to 90).

Let \(\tilde{\mathbf A} = \mathbf U\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf V^T\) with singular values \(s_1\ge s_2\ge\dots\). The naive solution \(\mathbf x = \sum_i (\mathbf u_i^T\tilde{\mathbf b}/s_i)\,\mathbf v_i\) divides the noise component of \(\mathbf u_i^T\tilde{\mathbf b}\) by singular values that decay to \(10^{-15}\) (condition number \(\sim10^{16}\) for the \(j_0\) kernel on test.dat). Regularization replaces \(1/s_i\) by filtered values.

Tikhonov regularization

General form

(1)\[\mathbf x_\lambda = \arg\min_{\mathbf x}\; \chi^2(\mathbf x) + \lambda^2\|\mathbf L\mathbf x\|^2 = \left(\tilde{\mathbf A}^T\tilde{\mathbf A} + \lambda^2\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\right)^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf A}^T\tilde{\mathbf b}.\]
Standard form (tikhonov_standard_gcv)

\(\mathbf L=\mathbf I\); in the SVD basis the filter factors are

\[f_i = \frac{s_i^2}{s_i^2+\lambda^2},\qquad \mathbf x_\lambda = \sum_i f_i\,\frac{\mathbf u_i^T\tilde{\mathbf b}}{s_i}\,\mathbf v_i .\]
Truncated SVD (tsvd_gcv)

\(f_i = 1\) for \(i\le k\), 0 otherwise.

General form (general_tikhonov_gcv)

\(\mathbf L\) is the second-difference operator below. A rank-deficient \(\mathbf L\in\mathbb R^{p\times N}\) is handled by Hansen’s QR-based transformation to standard form (std_form, method 1 of Regularization Tools): with \(\mathbf L^T = \mathbf K\mathbf R\) (\(\mathbf K=[\mathbf K_p,\mathbf K_0]\), \(\mathbf K_0\) spanning the null space of \(\mathbf L\)) and \(\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf K_0=\mathbf H\mathbf T\),

\[\mathbf L_p^\dagger = \mathbf K_p\mathbf R^{-T},\quad \bar{\mathbf A} = \mathbf H_2^T\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf L_p^\dagger,\quad \bar{\mathbf b} = \mathbf H_2^T\tilde{\mathbf b},\]

the standard-form problem in \(\bar{\mathbf x}\) is solved with filter factors and mapped back by \(\mathbf x = \mathbf L_p^\dagger\bar{\mathbf x} + \mathbf K_0\mathbf T^{-1}\mathbf H_1^T(\tilde{\mathbf b}-\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf L_p^\dagger\bar{\mathbf x})\). The result agrees with a direct solve of the normal equations to \(10^{-11}\).

Smoothness penalties

Let \(p_1\dots p_N\) be the unknowns (grid values or spline coefficients).

Second difference, free ends (second_derivative)
\[S_2(\mathbf p) = \sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\left(p_{j-1}-2p_j+p_{j+1}\right)^2 = \|\mathbf L_2\mathbf p\|^2,\qquad \mathbf L_2\in\mathbb R^{(N-2)\times N}.\]

The null space of \(\mathbf L_2\) contains constants and linear ramps: these components are not penalized at all. Together with the ill-conditioned \(j_0\) kernel this gave \(\chi^2_r\) in the hundreds on real data with GCV or the L-curve.

Hansen (2000), matrix as printed (hansen_printed)

The text after Hansen’s eq. 19 gives the tridiagonal matrix \(a_{ii}=1\), \(a_{i,i\pm1}=-\tfrac12\), which is the Hessian of

\[S_{pr}(\mathbf p) = \tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(p_j-p_{j+1})^2 + \tfrac12 p_1^2 + \tfrac12 p_N^2 ,\]

a first-difference penalty plus the assumption that \(p\) vanishes just outside \([0,D_{max}]\). It has full rank with \(\det = (N+1)/2^N\) (checked at construction).

Hansen (2000) eq. 19 (hansen_eq19, default)
(2)\[S_{19}(\mathbf p) = \sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\left[p_j-\tfrac12(p_{j-1}+p_{j+1})\right]^2 + \tfrac12 p_1^2 + \tfrac12 p_N^2 ,\]

a curvature penalty with the same boundary terms; its Hessian \(\mathbf D^T\mathbf D + \tfrac12(\mathbf e_1\mathbf e_1^T+\mathbf e_N\mathbf e_N^T)\) (\(\mathbf D\) with rows \((-\tfrac12,1,-\tfrac12)\)) is five-diagonal and positive definite. The printed matrix and eq. 19 are therefore not the same penalty; both are offered. The normalized evidence (below) prefers eq. 19 on test.dat (\(\ln Z=-153.0\) vs \(-236.2\) on the 95-point grid, \(-118.8\) vs \(-134.1\) with 20 B-splines), which made it the default.

Eq. 19 with \(p(0)\) free (hansen_eq19_even)

For the lamella kernel, whose \(p_t(0)>0\) and \(p_t'(0)=0\) (Forward model, data and information content), the curvature term at \(r=0\) uses the mirror point \(p_0=p_2\) of the even extension:

\[S_{19,e}(\mathbf p) = (p_1-p_2)^2 + \sum_{j=2}^{N-1}\left[p_j-\tfrac12(p_{j-1}+p_{j+1})\right]^2 + \tfrac12 p_N^2 .\]

Only \(p(D_{max})=0\) is imposed. The Hessian is positive definite (the only function with zero curvature, zero slope at 0 and \(p_N=0\) is zero), so the normalized evidence exists. This is the “free end point” option of Hansen (2016).

In all four cases \(\mathbf L\) is a factor with \(\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\) = Hessian of \(S\) (Cholesky factor for the Hansen penalties).

Bayesian evidence (bayesian_evidence)

Following Hansen (2000) and Vestergaard & Hansen (2006) the regularization is read as a Gaussian prior and the likelihood as Gaussian noise:

\[P(\mathbf p\mid\lambda) \propto \exp\!\left(-\lambda S(\mathbf p)\right), \qquad P(\mathbf d\mid\mathbf p) \propto \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12\chi^2(\mathbf p)\right), \qquad S(\mathbf p) = \mathbf p^T\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\,\mathbf p .\]
Posterior maximum (MAP)
(3)\[\mathbf p_\lambda = \arg\min\ \lambda S + \tfrac12\chi^2 = \left(2\lambda\mathbf L^T\mathbf L + \mathbf B\right)^{-1}\mathbf A^T\mathbf W\mathbf b , \qquad \mathbf B = \mathbf A^T\mathbf W\mathbf A .\]

(\(\mathbf B\) is the Hessian of \(\chi^2/2\), \(\mathbf{A}_S = 2\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\) that of \(S\).)

Evidence

Integrating the posterior over \(\mathbf p\) (exact for this linear-Gaussian model) gives the probability of the data for a given \(\lambda\):

(4)\[\ln P(\mathbf d\mid\lambda) = \tfrac12\ln\det\!\left(\lambda\mathbf A_S\right) - \tfrac12\ln\det\!\left(\lambda\mathbf A_S+\mathbf B\right) - \lambda S(\mathbf p_\lambda) - \tfrac12\chi^2(\mathbf p_\lambda) + \text{const}.\]

For the \(\lambda\) search the first term is replaced by \(\tfrac{N-1}{2}\ln\lambda\) (Vestergaard & Hansen eq. 6; the remaining \(\lambda\)-independent part of \(\det\mathbf A_S\) does not move the maximum). \(\lambda\) is the maximizer on a log grid of 60 values spanning \(10^{\pm6}\,\mathrm{median}(s_i)^2\) (\(s_i\) singular values of \(\mathbf A\)).

Normalized evidence (penalty comparison)

To compare different penalties, the full prior normalization must be kept:

\[\ln Z = \tfrac12\ln\det(2\lambda\mathbf L^T\mathbf L) - \tfrac12\ln\det(2\lambda\mathbf L^T\mathbf L+\mathbf B) - \lambda S - \tfrac12\chi^2 .\]

It is reported in the result line. For the second difference \(\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\) is singular (improper prior) and \(\ln Z\) is not defined.

Unknown error scale (noise_scale="estimate")

If the errors are known only up to a common factor, \(\sigma_i\to s\,\sigma_i\), the likelihood normalization adds \(-M\ln s\) to (4) and \(s\) is a second hyperparameter. pysasfit.inversion.solver_registry.estimate_noise_scale() maximizes

\[E(s) = \max_\lambda \ln P(\mathbf d\mid\lambda, s\boldsymbol\sigma) - M\ln s\]

over \(\ln s\in[\ln 0.01,\ln 100]\) (bounded scalar search, the inner maximum on an 80-point \(\lambda\) grid over 16 decades) and then solves with \(s\boldsymbol\sigma\). This is the Bayesian analogue of estimating the noise level from the residuals corrected for the number of fitted parameters (MacKay: at the optimum \(\chi^2(s\boldsymbol\sigma)=M-\gamma\), \(\gamma\) the effective number of parameters determined by the data). test.dat: with \(3\Delta I\) or \(\Delta I/3\) the estimate is \(s=0.323\) and \(2.91\) (product 0.97), and \(p(r)\) is identical to the one with the file errors (correlation 1.0000); with errors from the local cubic fits \(s=0.91\). \(\chi^2_r\) is reported for the unscaled \(\sigma\).

Fast evaluation over \(\lambda\)

With \(\mathbf P=\mathbf L^T\mathbf L\) positive definite (all Hansen penalties) one Cholesky factor \(\mathbf P=\mathbf R^T\mathbf R\) and one eigen-decomposition \(\mathbf R^{-T}\mathbf B\mathbf R^{-1}=\mathbf V\,\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\,\mathbf V^T\) give, for every \(\lambda\),

\[\ln\det(2\lambda\mathbf P+\mathbf B) = \ln\det\mathbf P + \sum_j\ln(2\lambda+\mu_j),\qquad \mathbf p_\lambda = \mathbf R^{-1}\mathbf V\, \mathrm{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda+\mu_j}\right) \mathbf V^T\mathbf R^{-T}\mathbf A^T\mathbf W\mathbf b ,\]

i.e. \(O(MN)\) work per \(\lambda\) instead of a solve, a determinant and an eigen-decomposition (pysasfit.inversion.solvers.bayesian_evidence.FastEvidence). Every \(\lambda\) is checked through the residual of its normal equations: where \(\mathbf P\) is ill-conditioned (e.g. the “p(0) free” penalty, which pins only one end) the decomposition loses digits at small \(\lambda\), and those \(\lambda\) are evaluated directly (one Cholesky factorization of \(2\lambda\mathbf P+\mathbf B\), with the \(\lambda\)-independent parts precomputed). On test.dat (\(j_0\), 95 points) the result is identical (\(|\Delta\ln Z|<5\cdot10^{-4}\) on the grid, same \(\lambda\)), and the evidence solver with estimated error scale runs in 0.4–0.6 s instead of 3–3.3 s.

Number of good parameters
\[N_g = \sum_j \frac{\mu_j}{\lambda+\mu_j},\]

with \(\mu_j\) the eigenvalues of \(\mathbf B\) (the \(\mathbf A_S=\mathbf I\) form of Vestergaard & Hansen’s eq. 8), an estimate of the number of parameters determined by the data; compare with \(N_s\) of (7).

Posterior covariance

The Laplace approximation, exact here, gives \(\mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf p) = (\lambda\mathbf A_S+\mathbf B)^{-1}\), shown as the analytic error band (Uncertainty of the recovered distribution).

Discrepancy principle

Choose the regularization parameter \(\theta\) (\(\lambda\), \(\lambda^2\), the EM smoothing weight \(h\) or the entropy weight) such that

\[\chi^2_r(\theta) = 1 ,\]

i.e. the residuals are as large as the stated errors. Two searches are implemented (pysasfit.inversion.solvers.lambda_search):

  • discrepancy_principle_search starts over-smoothed and walks \(\theta\) down geometrically until \(\chi^2_r-1\) changes sign, then refines by Brent’s method (EM + smoothing).

  • discrepancy_principle_search_bidirectional decides the direction from the sign of \(\chi^2_r-1\) at the start value, walks geometrically until the root is bracketed and refines by Brent. If the target is not reachable (plateau of \(\chi^2_r\) for four steps, or the search range is exhausted) the closest achievable solution is returned with reachable=False and a warning in the result line, stating which side failed: a too-small basis (cannot fit) or overestimated errors (always fits better than the target).

For the Bayesian-evidence solver “Force \(\chi^2_r=1\)” starts at the evidence-maximizing \(\lambda_{ev}\) and allows \(\lambda\) to decrease to at most \(10^{-3}\lambda_{ev}\). Reaching \(\chi^2_r=1\) only by almost removing the regularization means that the target is wrong for these data (underestimated \(\sigma\), or bootstrap replicates with inflated noise) and would give an oscillating, unregularized \(p(r)\).

When the discrepancy principle fails for EM + smoothing on a point grid, the L-curve corner is used instead; in B-spline mode \(h=0\) is used (the basis regularizes already, and the L-curve picks far too much extra smoothing there).

L-curve

For a decreasing sequence \(\theta_k = \theta_0/1.2^k\) the points \(P_k = (\ln\rho_k, \ln\eta_k)\) with residual measure \(\rho=\chi^2_r\) and roughness \(\eta=\sum_j(x_{j+1}-x_j)^2\) are computed, and the corner is the point of maximum Menger curvature of consecutive triples,

\[\kappa(P_{k-1},P_k,P_{k+1}) = \frac{4\,\mathcal A(P_{k-1},P_k,P_{k+1})} {|P_{k-1}P_k|\,|P_kP_{k+1}|\,|P_{k-1}P_{k+1}|},\]

\(\mathcal A\) the triangle area. This mirrors the Lcorner branch of SASfit’s DR_EM_smoothing.

Generalized cross-validation

GCV chooses \(\lambda\) (or the TSVD rank \(k\)) by minimizing

\[G(\lambda) = \frac{\|\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf x_\lambda-\tilde{\mathbf b}\|^2} {\left(M - \sum_i f_i\right)^2},\]

the leave-one-out prediction error in rotation-invariant form (for the general form evaluated in the standard-form variables, for TSVD with \(\sum_i f_i = k\)). Grid: 100 values from \(10^{-3}s_{min}\) to \(10\,s_{max}\); TSVD \(k\le40\).

ARLS (arlsnn)

Jones’ automatically regularized least squares uses the discrete Picard condition: the Tikhonov coefficients \(g_i(\lambda) = \beta_i s_i/(s_i^2+\lambda^2)\), \(\boldsymbol\beta=\mathbf U^T\tilde{\mathbf b}\), should decay. The smallest \(\lambda\) for which a line and a parabola fitted to \(\log_{10}|g_i|\) both have non-positive slope is taken, then \(\lambda\) is increased as long as the residual stays below twice its value. Non-negativity: columns with negative coefficients are removed repeatedly. No parameter has to be chosen; it serves as a quick sanity check.
