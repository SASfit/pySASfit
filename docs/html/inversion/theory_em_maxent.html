EM, maximum entropy and signed kernels

Expectation–maximization (Richardson–Lucy)

For a non-negative kernel \(A_{ij}\ge0\) and non-negative unknowns, the EM algorithm maximizes the Poisson log-likelihood

\[\ell(\mathbf x) = \sum_i \left[ b_i\ln(\mathbf A\mathbf x)_i - (\mathbf A\mathbf x)_i\right],\]

equivalently it minimizes the Kullback–Leibler divergence \(\mathrm{KL}(\mathbf b\,\|\,\mathbf A\mathbf x)\). The stationarity condition \(\partial\ell/\partial x_j = 0\) for \(x_j>0\),

\[\sum_i A_{ij}\frac{b_i}{(\mathbf A\mathbf x)_i} = \sum_i A_{ij},\]

is turned into the multiplicative fixed-point iteration (Kohlbrecher & Bressler 2022, eq. 36–37)

(1)\[x_j^{(k+1)} = x_j^{(k)}\; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_i A_{ij}\, b_i/(\mathbf A\mathbf x^{(k)})_i} {\displaystyle\sum_i A_{ij}} .\]

Each step keeps \(\mathbf x\ge0\) and does not decrease \(\ell\). Columns with \(\sum_iA_{ij}=0\) (e.g. \(r=0\) with \(\alpha>0\)) are left unchanged. The iteration converges slowly and, run to convergence without regularization, ends in a noisy maximum likelihood solution; early stopping acts as an uncontrolled regularizer (“semi-convergence”). All EM solvers here therefore run to convergence and regularize explicitly.

Stopping criterion: \(\|\mathbf x^{(k+1)}-\mathbf x^{(k)}\|_2\le\) tol (SASfit’s gNorm), for the accelerated iteration the relative change of \(\|\mathbf x\|\).

EM + smoothing (em_discrepancy)

After every EM step the solution is smoothed with the tridiagonal operator (eq. 41–46)

\[\begin{split}\mathbf S^{(h)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1-h & h \\ h & 1-2h & h\\ & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots\\ && h & 1-2h & h\\ &&& h & 1-h\end{pmatrix},\qquad \mathbf x^{(k+1)} = \mathbf S^{(h)}\,\mathrm{EM}(\mathbf x^{(k)}),\end{split}\]

\(0<h\lesssim0.3\); rows sum to one, so the smoothing conserves \(\sum_jx_j\) and positivity. (The optional double smoothing also smooths \(\ln\mathbf x\) before the step, \(\mathbf x\leftarrow\exp(\mathbf S^{(h)}\ln\mathbf x)\).) The fixed point balances the EM correction against the diffusion-like smoothing; \(h\) plays the role of \(\lambda\). It is chosen by the discrepancy principle (start \(h=0.3\), floor \(10^{-9}\)) or the L-curve corner.

Biggs–Andrews acceleration

All EM-type iterations are accelerated by vector extrapolation (Biggs & Andrews 1997; pysasfit.inversion.solvers.acceleration.biggs_andrews(), ported from SASfit’s fixed-point solver). With the fixed-point map \(\mathcal T\) and the last updates \(\mathbf g_{n} = \mathcal T(\mathbf y_{n})-\mathbf y_{n}\):

\[\alpha_n = \frac{\mathbf g_{n-1}\cdot\mathbf g_{n-2}}{\mathbf g_{n-2}\cdot\mathbf g_{n-2}}, \qquad |\alpha_n|\le\frac{n-1}{n+2},\]
\[\mathbf y_n = \mathbf x_n + \alpha_n(\mathbf x_n-\mathbf x_{n-1}) + \tfrac12\alpha_n^2(\mathbf x_n-2\mathbf x_{n-1}+\mathbf x_{n-2}), \qquad \mathbf x_{n+1} = \mathcal T(\mathbf y_n),\]

(second order, kin_set_maa=2; order 1 drops the last term, order 0 is plain Picard iteration). For non-negative problems \(\mathbf y_n\) is clipped to \(\ge0\) before \(\mathcal T\) is applied. The fixed point is unchanged; on the JAC 2022 benchmark the iteration count drops from more than 200 000 to about 2 800.

EM + MaxEnt (maxent_em)

SASfit’s DR_EM_ME (Kohlbrecher & Bressler 2022, eq. 47–55) adds an entropy correction to each EM step,

\[S(\mathbf x,\mathbf m) = \sum_j\left[-x_j\ln\frac{x_j}{m_j} + x_j - m_j\right], \qquad \mathbf x^{(k+1)} = \mathbf x^{(k)} + \Delta\mathbf x^{(k)}_{EM} + \Delta\mathbf s^{(k)} .\]
Constant prior

\(m_j=\bar b/N\) fixed and

\[\Delta s_j = \lambda\,x_j\left[-\ln\frac{x_j}{m_j} + \frac{\sum_l x_l\ln(x_l/m_l)}{\sum_l x_l}\right],\]

the gradient of \(S\) in the EM metric (multiplied by \(x_j\)), projected such that \(\sum_j\Delta s_j=0\).

Adaptive prior

The prior is the current solution smoothed with a Gaussian point spread function (Horne 1985),

\[m_i = \sum_j\Pi_{ij}x_j,\qquad \Pi_{ij} = \frac{1}{c_i}\exp\!\left[-\frac{(i-j)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right], \quad\sum_j\Pi_{ij}=1,\]

(\(\sigma^2=1\) grid step squared) and

\[\Delta s_j = \lambda\,x_j\left[\frac{\sum_l x_l\ln(x_l/m_l)}{\sum_l x_l} - \ln\frac{x_j}{m_j} - 1 + \sum_i\Pi_{ij}\frac{x_i}{m_i}\right].\]

Entropy relative to a smoothed version of itself acts as a smoothness prior.

\(\lambda\) is chosen from 24 values between \(10^{-9}\) and 1 as the one with \(\chi^2_r\) closest to 1, every trial run to convergence.

Maximum entropy, Hansen’s functional (hansen_maxent)

Following Hansen & Elfving (maxent.m of Regularization Tools) but with statistical weights and a flat default model \(m\):

(2)\[F(\mathbf x) = \sum_i\frac{\left[(\mathbf A\mathbf x)_i-b_i\right]^2}{\sigma_i^2} + \lambda^2\sum_j x_j\ln\frac{x_j}{m},\qquad x_j>0 .\]
\[\nabla F = 2\tilde{\mathbf A}^T(\tilde{\mathbf A}\mathbf x-\tilde{\mathbf b}) + \lambda^2\left(1+\ln\frac{\mathbf x}{m}\right),\qquad \nabla^2F = 2\tilde{\mathbf A}^T\tilde{\mathbf A} + \lambda^2\,\mathrm{diag}(1/x_j).\]

  • Default model: the constant that best fits the data, \(m = \frac{\mathbf a^T\tilde{\mathbf b}}{\mathbf a^T\mathbf a}\), \(a_i = \sum_j\tilde A_{ij}\).

  • Minimization: bound-constrained L-BFGS-B (\(x_j\ge10^{-30}m\)) in variables scaled by \(s_j=\sqrt{(\nabla^2F)_{jj}}\) at \(\mathbf x=m\), followed by a Newton polish on the free components (fraction-to-boundary 0.99, Armijo backtracking). Without the polish L-BFGS-B stops in the flat valley of the ill-conditioned \(j_0\) problem with \(\chi^2_r\) off by several percent and non-monotonic in \(\lambda\).

  • \(\lambda^2\) by the bidirectional discrepancy principle, warm- starting each trial from the previous solution.

The original non-linear CG transcription of maxent.m (kept as hansen_maxent.solve) caps each step where the first component reaches zero and has no active-set handling; on test.dat it stopped after 2–18 iterations at \(F=71\,049\) where the minimum of the same functional is \(F=165\).

EM for signed kernels (em_general_signed_kernel)

The \(j_0\) kernel and signed \(p(r)\) violate both assumptions of (1). Chae, Martin & Walker (2019, Sec. 6) reduce the general problem to a non-negative one in two steps.

Shift (signed solution, their eq. 10)

With a constant \(t>\max|p|\),

\[\tilde p = p + t \ge 0,\qquad \tilde b_i = b_i + t\sum_j |A_{ij}| ,\]
Kernel split (signed kernel, their eq. 12)

\(\mathbf A = \mathbf A^+ - \mathbf A^-\) with \(\mathbf A^\pm\ge0\) (pointwise positive and negative parts), extended kernel and unknown

\[\begin{split}\mathbf K = \left[\mathbf A^+\ \ \mathbf A^-\right]\in\mathbb R^{M\times2N}_{\ge0}, \qquad \mathbf u = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf p + t\\ -\mathbf p + t\end{pmatrix}\ge0, \qquad \mathbf K\mathbf u = \tilde{\mathbf b} .\end{split}\]

EM (1) is applied to \((\mathbf K,\tilde{\mathbf b})\). The extended problem has more solutions than those of structure \([\mathbf p,-\mathbf p]\), so after each step the structure is restored from the first half, \(\mathbf p\leftarrow\mathbf u_{1:N}-t\) (an antisymmetrized variant is available). \(|p_j|\) is clipped to \(0.999\,t\). Default \(t = 10\max_j|p^{ARLS}_j|\) from a quick ARLS estimate; results are nearly independent of \(t\) once it exceeds \(\max|p|\).

\(\chi^2\) row weighting (2026-10-10)

EM minimizes \(\mathrm{KL}(\tilde{\mathbf b}\|\mathbf K\mathbf u) \approx\sum_i(\mathbf K\mathbf u-\tilde{\mathbf b})_i^2/(2\tilde b_i)\): the implicit weights are \(1/\tilde b_i\). After the shift \(\tilde b_i\approx t\sum_j|A_{ij}|\) is dominated by the shift; for \(j_0\) the row sums do not decay with \(q\), and the real signal at high \(q\) is only \(10^{-5}\)–\(10^{-4}\) of \(\tilde b_i\) – EM is blind to these data (test.dat: \(\chi^2_r\ge5200\) without smoothing, for any \(t\)). Scaling each row by

\[w_i = \frac{\tilde b_i}{\sigma_i^2}:\qquad \mathbf K\to\mathrm{diag}(w)\mathbf K,\quad \tilde{\mathbf b}\to\mathrm{diag}(w)\tilde{\mathbf b},\]

keeps the kernel non-negative and changes the misfit to

\[\sum_i\frac{w_i^2(\mathbf K\mathbf u-\tilde{\mathbf b})_i^2}{2w_i\tilde b_i} = \sum_i\frac{(\mathbf K\mathbf u-\tilde{\mathbf b})_i^2}{2\sigma_i^2} = \tfrac12\chi^2(\mathbf p),\]

because \(\mathbf K\mathbf u-\tilde{\mathbf b}=\mathbf A\mathbf p-\mathbf b\). The fixed point then fits the data within their errors.

Acceleration safeguard

With the first-half rebuild the iteration is no longer a monotone EM map, and Biggs–Andrews extrapolation can drift (13 spline coefficients: \(\chi^2_r\) 1.68 to 260). If the accelerated run ends unconverged with \(\chi^2_r>1.05\times\) the best iterate, plain Picard iteration continues from the best iterate (acceleration_fallback in the diagnostics).

Regularization options

smoothing – \(\mathbf p\leftarrow\mathbf S^{(h)}\mathbf p\) after each step, \(h\) by the discrepancy principle (floor \(10^{-7}\)).

MaxEnt – the entropy of a signed \(p\) is defined through the two-channel representation \(p = h_+ - h_-\), \(h_\pm\ge0\), with default model \(m\) for both channels (Skilling & Gull); eliminating the channels gives

\[S_\pm(\mathbf p) = \sum_j\left[\psi_j - 2m - p_j\,\mathrm{asinh}\frac{p_j}{2m}\right], \qquad \psi_j=\sqrt{p_j^2+4m^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial S_\pm}{\partial p_j} = -\mathrm{asinh}\frac{p_j}{2m}.\]

In analogy to SASfit’s \(\Delta s = \lambda\,x\,\partial S/\partial x\) the metric \(\psi_j\) is used, and each EM step is followed by

\[\begin{split}p_j \leftarrow p_j + \lambda\,\psi_j\left[\tau_j - \mathrm{asinh}\frac{p_j}{2m}\right], \qquad \tau_j = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{constant prior}\\ \mathrm{asinh}\dfrac{(\boldsymbol\Pi\mathbf p)_j}{2m} & \text{adaptive prior}\end{cases}\end{split}\]

For \(|p|\ll m\) this is a ridge pull \(-\lambda p\) (towards 0 or towards \(\boldsymbol\Pi\mathbf p\)), for \(|p|\gg m\) the usual MaxEnt behaviour \(-\lambda|p|\ln(|p|/m)\). Default \(m=0.1\max|p^{ARLS}|\); \(\lambda\) by the bidirectional discrepancy principle (start \(10^{-5}\), range \(10^{-12}\)–1). A first attempt applying SASfit’s correction to the shifted \(\mathbf u\) with prior \(t\) failed: the entropy then degenerates to a ridge with scale \(t\), and the adaptive variant diverged.
