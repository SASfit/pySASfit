pySEBpolymers: API reference

Generated from the docstrings.

Structures built from linked sub-units and their scattering.

Formalism (Svaneborg & Pedersen, J. Chem. Phys. 136, 104105 (2012)): for a tree of sub-units I with excess scattering lengths beta_I,

S(q) = sum_I beta_I^2 F_I

  • sum_{I != J} beta_I beta_J A_{I,a} [prod_K Psi_{K,b,c}] A_{J,d}

where the product runs over the sub-units K on the path between I and J, entering K at reference point b and leaving at c; a and d are the reference points through which the path leaves I and enters J. The normalised form factor is S / (sum_I beta_I)^2.

The double sum is evaluated with a tree recursion in O(N) (instead of O(N^2) pairs), so dendrimers and brushes with thousands of sub-units are cheap. form_factor_pairs is the direct O(N^2) sum, kept for testing.

class pysasfit.seb.structure.Structure(name='structure')[source]

A flat structure: named sub-units joined at reference points.

Reference points are written "name.ref" or, for distributed references, "name.ref#label": the same label means the same point, different labels independent random points. An unlabelled distributed reference gets a fresh label (a new random point).

Parameters of sub-unit name with tag t (default t = name) are beta_t and its size parameters Rg_t, R_t, L_t …; sub-units sharing a tag share parameters.

add(kind, name, tag=None, model=None, poly=False, thick=False, copy=())[source]

Add a sub-unit. kind is a type name (see REGISTRY) or a SubUnit instance (custom sub-unit or non-default quadrature).

poly: Schulz-Zimm polydisperse in mass, relative width sigma_<tag> (sizes scale as in MASS_SCALING, beta with the mass); thick: cylindrical cross-section of radius Rc_<tag> (chain-like sub-units, THICK_KINDS); copy: [(group, index, parameter), …] – the sub-unit belongs to copy index of the group (outermost first); copies of a group declared with interact() see each other.

interact(param)[source]

Excluded volume between the copies of the groups whose copy labels name param (e.g. crowded micelle coronas): a correlation hole in the relative positions of two copies. Every interference term between sub-units I, J of different copies becomes

beta_I beta_J [(1 + k) (A … Psi … A) - k F_c(q)], k = phi / (n - 1),

phi = vex_<param> in [0, 1) the strength of the hole (the n - 1 other copies together remove up to the fraction phi of one copy’s own scattering), F_c the normalised scattering of one copy (all its sub-units, averaged over the copies). At q = 0 every term is unchanged, so the forward scattering keeps its exact value; at large q the interference of a group is at least -phi times its self terms, so the intensity stays positive.

Designs: structures drawn as graphs of sub-units, patterns and components (the pySEBpolymers designer).

A Design is a graph whose nodes are

  • sub-units (type="unit"): one sub-unit of the library (GaussianPolymer, SolidSphere, …); its ports are the reference points of that sub-unit;

  • patterns, which stand for many sub-units:

    • Array: n copies of an element, each attached by the element’s in-port to the point the array’s anchor port is linked to – at independent random points of a distributed host point (mode="independent", e.g. chains on a surface) or all at the same point (mode="same");

    • Chain: n copies joined out-port -> in-port; ports start and end;

    • Generations: a dendrimer, f elements at the root point and f - 1 new elements at the out-port of every element of the previous generation, g generations (optionally one tag per generation);

    the element is a sub-unit type or a component;

  • components are designs of their own with an in port (and for chains and generations an out port), each on a sub-unit node of the component, e.g. a bottle-brush segment (backbone block + side chain at its end).

Links join two ports. On a distributed port (surface, contour, hull …) every link has a label: the same label on the same node is the same point, different labels are independent random points. The node graph must be a tree (no loops); Design.link() refuses links that would close one.

Design.to_code() writes Python code that builds the Structure s with loops for the patterns and one function per component; the panel builds the structure from that code, so designs, scripts and “Copy Python code” are always the same thing. Design.from_structure() draws an existing structure.

A random 3D realisation of a structure, for looking at it (the “3D sketch” panel).

The scattering formalism averages over conformations, orientations and the random points of distributed references; realise() draws ONE such realisation: Gaussian chains and loops as random walks with the right radius of gyration, rods as straight lines in random directions, solid bodies at random orientations, random points on surfaces and contours (one per label). Excluded volume is ignored, as in the model. draw() puts it into a matplotlib 3D axes, one colour per tag.

Sub-unit library.

Every sub-unit provides, as functions of the scattering vector q,

  • ff – normalised form factor F(q) (pair-distance average of sin(q r)/(q r) over all pairs of scatterers),

  • amp – form-factor amplitude A_ref(q) relative to a reference point,

  • psi – phase factor Psi_{r1,r2}(q) between two reference points.

For a distributed reference point (random point on a surface/contour), psi(q, p, "surface", "surface") is the phase factor between two independent random points of that distribution.

Expressions are the standard ones of the small-angle scattering literature (Debye 1947; Hammouda 1992; Guinier/Fournet; Svaneborg & Pedersen, J. Chem. Phys. 136, 104105 & 154907 (2012)). Reference-point and parameter names follow the SEB / pyseb conventions so structures are interchangeable.

Linear chains given by their two-point function.

For a homogeneous linear chain of contour length L the scattering depends only on the two-point function G(q, s) = <sin(q r)/(q r)> of two contour points a contour distance s apart. With t = s/L:

F = 2 int_0^1 (1 - t) G(tL) dt A_end = int_0^1 G(tL) dt Psi(end1, end2) = G(L) A_middle = 2 int_0^1/2 G(tL) dt Psi(end, middle) = G(L/2) Psi(contour, end) = A_end, Psi(contour, middle) = A_middle, Psi(contour, contour) = F

(all reference points of GaussianPolymer). The integrals are done by composite Gauss-Legendre quadrature on a grid in z = s/L that resolves the oscillations of G at large q.

Models:

  • WormLikeChain – Kholodenko’s worm (A. L. Kholodenko, Macromolecules 26 (1993) 4179): contour length L, Kuhn length b; the rod limit for s << b and the Gaussian limit (Kuhn length b) for s >> b are exact; in between it is Kholodenko’s approximation, with <R^2(s)> = (b^2/3)(z coth z - 1), z = 3 s/b (the worm-like chain has b s - (b^2/2)(1 - exp(-2 s/b)); Kholodenko’s is up to 20 % larger around s ~ 0.7 b, < 1 % for s > 20 b).

  • WormLikeChainEV – worm-like chain with excluded volume: the form factor of Pedersen & Schurtenberger (Macromolecules 29 (1996) 7602, method 3 with excluded volume; corrections of Chen et al., Langmuir 22 (2006) 6539), the other quantities from its dependence on L (ChainFromF); for L < 2.5 b it crosses over smoothly to Kholodenko’s worm (PS is not valid for L < b).

  • ExcludedVolumePolymer – excluded-volume chain with non-Gaussian distances (des Cloizeaux, J. Phys. (Paris) 41 (1980) 223; Foerster & Burger, Macromolecules 31 (1998) 879): distances with P(r) ~ r^(2+theta) exp(-(D r)^delta), delta = 1/(1 - nu), <r^2(s)> ~ s^(2 nu); parameters Rg, nu. The correlation-hole exponent depends on where the two points are: theta_0 = (gamma - 1)/nu = 0.275 between the two ends, theta_1 = 0.46 between an end and an interior point, theta_2 = 0.71 between two interior points (self-avoiding walk values, scaled by (nu - 1/2)/(0.588 - 1/2) for other nu, so nu = 1/2 is the Gaussian chain). F and the amplitude about the middle use theta_2, the amplitude about an end and Psi(end, middle) theta_1, Psi(end1, end2) theta_0.
