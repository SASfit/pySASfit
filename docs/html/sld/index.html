pySLDcalculator

Scattering length densities of materials and biomolecules for neutrons and X-rays, with the quantities one needs around them: molecular volume, electron density, cross sections and transmissions, the contrast to a matrix, the excess scattering length, and the D₂O match point.

pysasfit-sld                                   # the window (also a tab of pysasfit-gui)
pysasfit-sld SiO2 -d 2.2                       # values on the command line
pysasfit-sld "C8H8@1.05" --matrix "C7D8@0.943"
pysasfit-sld --aa KVFGRCELAAAMKR... --d2o 0.4
pip install pysasfit[sld]                      # periodictable and xraydb

Input

Formula in the grammar of periodictable (the NIST calculator): SiO2, C6H5CH3, Pd1.3Si, CaCO3 + 6H2O; isotopes D2O, C7D8, Ca[44]CO3; mixtures 3%wt NaCl // H2O. Exchangeable (labile) hydrogen is written H[1]: C2H5OH[1].

Size: the mass density (g/cm³) of the formula as written, or the volume of one formula unit (Å³). They are related by V = M / (ρ N_A). The volume is kept for all isotopic variants (deuteration, H/D exchange); the density then follows from the mass.

Densities are usually known for the protonated material. For a deuterated one either

  • write the protonated formula with its density and set the deuteration (0 … 1), or

  • write the deuterated formula and tick density is that of the protonated material (Python: natural_density=True, command line -n, in a formula C7D8@0.867n), or

  • give the measured density of the deuterated material.

A deuterated formula with the protonated density and without the tick gets a volume that is too large: for toluene-d8 with 0.867 g/cm³ the SLD would be 5.21 instead of 5.66 · 10⁻⁶ Å⁻².

Sequences: protein, DNA or RNA in one-letter codes, chains separated by / or new lines, FASTA headers ignored. The volume is the sum of the residue volumes, or is set by a density (use 1/v̄ for a known partial specific volume) or a volume.

Radiation: neutron wavelength; X-ray energy, wavelength or the Kα line of an anode (Cu, Mo, Ag, Ga, Cr, Co, Fe, In); X-ray table.

Matrix: an H₂O/D₂O mixture, vacuum, or one of the materials.

Searching a material

Search… (Ctrl+F, or pysasfit-sld --search toluene) looks a name up and lists formula, density, source and a note; a double click adds the hit to the materials.

source

content

built-in library

offline

common solvents (also deuterated), polymers, inorganic materials

xraydb materials

offline

about 90 entries: elements, solvents, minerals, window materials

ORSO SLD database

online

slddb.reflectometry.org: materials collected for reflectometry and SAS, with reference and validation flag; name or formula

Wikipedia

online

formula and density of the infobox of the English articles found for the name

PubChem

online

formula of a named compound and its experimental densities (handbook values with temperature and source); exact compound names work best

The densities of Wikipedia and PubChem are read from free text, which is shown in the note: look at it (temperature, phase, a range) before using the value. A hit without density is added with density 0. A source that cannot be reached is named below the table and does not stop the others.

from pysasfit.sld.lookup import search, hit_entry
hits, errors = search("toluene", sources=("builtin", "xraydb", "orso"))

Data

source

remarks

neutron scattering lengths, cross sections

periodictable: Rauch & Waschkowski (2003), Sears (1992)

per isotope; absorption ∝ wavelength; energy-dependent b of rare-earth isotopes (Lynn & Seeger 1990)

X-ray f′, f″ (default)

xraydb: Chantler

fine energy grid at the absorption edges, Z ≤ 98

X-ray f₁, f₂ (alternative)

periodictable: Henke, Gullikson & Davis (CXRO)

30 eV – 30 keV, Z ≤ 92

residue formulas and volumes

periodictable.fasta: Perkins (1988), volumes as in Jacrot (1976); nucleotides Buckin (1989), Durchschlag & Zipper (1997)

Asp, Glu ionised; Lys, Arg protonated

water

0.99821 (H₂O) and 1.10536 g/cm³ (D₂O) at 20 °C, ideal mixing

Both X-ray tables are for free atoms. Within some 10 eV of an edge the real f′, f″ depend on the chemical state (XANES) and should be measured. The X-ray SLD is the forward value (q = 0, f₀ = Z). The tabulated bound-atom incoherent cross sections underestimate the scattering of hydrogen for cold neutrons (H₂O: 5.6 cm⁻¹ calculated, about 7 cm⁻¹ measured), so real transmissions are lower.

Quantities

With V the volume of the formula unit, b = Σ nᵢ b_c,ᵢ (neutrons) or b = r_e Σ nᵢ (Z + f′ + i f″)ᵢ (X-rays, r_e = 2.818 fm):

SLD

ρ = b / V

contrast

Δρ = ρ − ρ₀ (ρ₀: matrix)

excess scattering length

Δb = b − ρ₀ V = Δρ V

forward scattering

I(0)/c = N_A (Δb)² / M (dilute particles of one formula unit, c in g/cm³)

X-rays

δ = λ² Re ρ / 2π, β = λ² Im ρ / 2π, μ = 4πβ/λ (photoabsorption)

critical q

q_c = 4 √(π ρ)

magnetic SLD

2.695 fm × (moment in μ_B) / V

Hydrogen exchange

In a solvent with the D₂O fraction x a labile hydrogen of class c is deuterium with the probability x·p_c:

class

default p

backbone amide N-H of proteins

0.9

one per residue, except proline and the first residue (Perkins 1986; DNEXCH = 0.1 of CRYSON)

all other labile H

1.0

side-chain and terminal N-H, O-H, S-H; nucleic acids; H[1] of formulas

H bound to carbon (written H)

—

does not exchange; the deuteration of the material replaces that fraction by D

For lysozyme this gives an exchanging fraction of 0.952 of its 264 labile hydrogens, the usual 0.95 of sequence-based calculators (MULCh, SCOMAP-XD), and a match point of 44.7 % D₂O. SLD of material and solvent are linear in x, so the match point is exact.

Python

from pysasfit.sld import Material, Probe, Exchange, water, contrast, match_point, energy_scan

probe = Probe(neutron_wavelength=6.0, xray_energy=8.048, xray_table="chantler")
silica = Material.from_formula("SiO2", density=2.2)
p = silica.properties(probe)
p.volume, p.n_sld, p.x_sld, p.x_mu                 # Å³, 1e-6 Å^-2, 1e-6 Å^-2, 1/cm
c = contrast(silica, water(0.4), probe)
c["n_drho"], c["n_excess"], c["x_excess_electrons"]

lys = Material.from_sequence("KVFGRCELAAAMKRHG...", kind="aa", deuteration=0.0)
match_point(lys, probe, Exchange(backbone=0.9, side=1.0))

import numpy as np
scan = energy_scan(Material.from_formula("Fe3O4", density=5.17), np.linspace(6.9, 7.3, 800))
scan["sld"], scan["sld_imag"], scan["edges"]
