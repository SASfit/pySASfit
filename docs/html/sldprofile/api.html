Direct estimation (Hansen 2016)

Direct Bayesian estimation of the radial density distribution.

Implementation of

S. Hansen, “Estimation of the density distribution from small-angle scattering data”, J. Appl. Cryst. 49 (2016) 856-865.

The radial scattering-length-density (contrast) profile rho(r) of a sphere, an infinitely long cylinder or an infinite lamella is estimated directly from I(q) (no intermediate p(r)), by minimising (eq. 12)

Phi(rho) = alpha S(rho) + chi^2(rho)/2, I_i = rho^T C_i rho (+ b)

with the smoothness (eq. 14) or maximum-entropy (eq. 15) functional S. Because the model is quadratic in rho, Phi is minimised with a damped Gauss-Newton (Levenberg-Marquardt) iteration.

The Lagrange multiplier alpha (overall noise level) and the maximum radius r_max (D = 2 r_max) are hyper-parameters estimated from their posterior probability (Hansen 2000, 2016), using the Gaussian approximation around the optimum:

log P(alpha, r_max) = -alpha S - chi^2/2 - 1/2 log det(alpha A + B)
                      + 1/2 log det(alpha A)

with A = grad grad S and B = J^T J (Gauss-Newton Hessian of chi^2/2).

To avoid the local minima mentioned by Hansen, alpha is decreased from a large value (where the solution is close to a homogeneous profile / the prior) towards small values along a continuation path, each solution being warm-started from the previous one.

adaptive_grid(r_max, beta=3.0, max_ratio=8.0, smoothing=2.0, solution=None, verbose=False, min_width='auto')[source]

Normalised shell edges equidistributing the variation of the represented profile.

A first, deliberately over-smoothed solution c(r) (alpha raised by smoothing e-folds above the evidence optimum, unless solution is given) defines the monitor:

M(r) = 1 + beta |c'(r)| / mean |c'|

(de Boor: for a piecewise-constant representation the error per shell is ~ dr abs(c’), so equal M-mass per shell equalises it). M is limited to max_ratio x its minimum, so shells are at most max_ratio times finer than the coarsest. min_width is the smallest allowed shell width in r units; 'auto' = min(pi/q_max, r_max/N): structure finer than the data resolution pi/q_max is not determined by I(q), and finer shells only add freedom that the prior has to suppress (lower evidence). Returns the grid; pass it to a new DensityProfileFit (grid=...) with otherwise identical settings.

Glatter’s two-step route

Two-step (indirect) estimation of the density profile, after Glatter.

Glatter’s route to rho(r) of a centro-symmetric scatterer takes two steps:

  1. Indirect Fourier transformation (Glatter 1977): I(q) -> p(r), the distance distribution of the sphere, of the cylinder cross-section or of the lamella thickness, with D_max = 2 r_max.

  2. Deconvolution (Glatter 1981, “DECON”): p(r) = rho^T V(r) rho is the self-convolution of the density profile; rho(r) is found by fitting this quadratic form to p(r) with a smoothness constraint.

Hansen (2016) replaces both steps by the direct estimate in DensityProfileFit. Both routes are offered here so they can be compared on the same data:

  • step 1 is a Bayesian IFT (Tikhonov, Hansen 2000 eq. 19 curvature penalty with p(0) = p(D_max) = 0, lambda by maximum evidence) from pysasfit.inversion, with the geometry-specific transform and the instrumental resolution; D_max (and optionally a flat background) is chosen by the evidence;

  • step 2 is DeconvolutionFit, the same Bayesian machinery as the direct fit (shells, bases, smoothness or max-ent, alpha by evidence), with the data I(q) replaced by p(r) and the forward model by the quadratic form p_j = rho^T V_j rho. r_max = D_max / 2 is fixed by step 1, as in Glatter’s method. The p(r) errors are by default only the standard deviations of step 1 (errors="diagonal"), as in Glatter’s DECON. errors="covariance" uses the full posterior covariance; it is not recommended: the covariance of the regularised IFT is nearly singular (condition number ~1e7 on 60 points) and in its small-variance directions p(r) is fixed by the smoothness prior, not by the data. Model differences there count enormously – even the exact p(r) of the true particle then gives chi2/M of 6-200 (homogeneous / core-shell sphere tests, 2026-10-21), against 0.2-1.4 with diagonal errors.

Transforms (normalisation identical to pysasfit.sldprofile.geometry.pddf()):

sphere

I(q) = 4 pi int p(r) sin(qr)/(qr) dr

cylinder

I(q) = (pi/q) 2 pi int p(r) J0(qr) dr (per unit length)

lamella

I(q) = (2 pi/q^2) 2 int p(r) cos(qr) dr (per unit area)

pysasfit.sldprofile.twostep.resample_pddf(pd, factor=4)[source]

(r, p, sigma) of the IFT p(r) on a factor times finer grid of [0, D_max].

pysasfit.sldprofile.twostep.ift_kernel(q, r, geometry='sphere', quadrature='hat')[source]

Matrix A with I(q_i) = sum_j A_ij p(r_j) for p on the uniform grid r (interior points of [0, D_max]; p(0) = p(D_max) = 0).

quadrature="hat" (default since 2026-10-21): p(r) is piecewise linear between the points (zero one step outside the grid) and the kernel is integrated on a sub-grid with at least 4 points per half period of the fastest oscillation at q_max, so the number of points can be as small as the Shannon number q_max D_max / pi. "points": rectangle rule K(q, r_j) dr (the former behaviour; needs ~3x more points for the same accuracy).

B-spline representation

B-spline representation of the density profile.

Instead of N piecewise-constant shells the represented quantity c(r) = r^p rho(r) (basis ‘rho’ -> p = 0, ‘mass’ -> p = dim-1, or a number) is a clamped B-spline of degree k on normalised breakpoints 0 = t_0 < … < t_m = 1 (x = r / r_max):

c(r) = sum_i c_i B_i(r / r_max),         rho(r) = c(r) / r^p   (r <= r_max)

Why: every shell boundary is a small step in rho and adds its own Porod-like high-q tail; with many shells their interference can fit noise at large q. A cubic spline is C^2 inside [0, r_max]; the only step left is the (physical) one at r_max when the outer end is free.

The amplitude stays linear in the coefficients, F(q) = sum_i a_i(q) c_i, with

a_i(q) = A_d int_0^r_max B_i(r/r_max) r^(d-1-p) K(qr) dr

(A_d, K as for the shells), integrated by Gauss quadrature on every knot span (Gauss-Jacobi with weight r^(d-1-p) on the first span, so that a centre cusp is integrated exactly). Everything else – the quadratic model, the evidence, the background, the resolution – is unchanged.

End conditions (hard, by the choice of the basis):

  • centre: the represented quantity is even through r = 0, c’(0) = 0, i.e. the first two coefficients of the clamped basis are tied (B_0 + B_1);

  • outer end 'free': c(r_max) free, a step to 0 at r_max (sharp surface); 'zero': c(r_max) = 0 (last basis function dropped).

Smoothness prior: S = (h^3/4) int_0^1 c’’(x)^2 dx with h = 1/N (N = number of coefficients) – the continuum limit of Hansen’s (2016) eq. 14 on a grid of N points, evaluated exactly from the B-splines for any knot placement.

Usage: pass grid=BSplines(degree=3, knots=...) to DensityProfileFit (or the two-step functions); n is then the number of spline coefficients.

Geometry and regularisation

Scattering amplitudes of symmetric density profiles (Hansen 2016, eqs. 1-5).

The radial density (scattering-length-density contrast) profile rho(r), 0 <= r <= r_max, is represented by N piecewise-constant shells with edges r_k = k * r_max / N. For each geometry the orientationally averaged intensity of a centro-symmetric scatterer is the square of a real amplitude that is linear in rho:

F(q) = sum_k a_k(q) rho_k ,          I(q) = c_dim(q) F(q)^2

geometry

a_k(q) = integral over shell k

c_dim(q)

sphere

4 pi int r^2 sin(qr)/(qr) dr

1

cylinder

2 pi int r J0(qr) dr (infinitely long)

pi / q

lamella

2 int cos(qx) dx (infinitely extended)

2 pi / q^2

cylinder is per unit length, lamella per unit area.

Hansen (2016) writes the same model as I_i = rho^T C_i rho (eq. 11) with C_ikl = sum_j A_ij V_jkl, i.e. via the distance distribution p(r) and the overlap integrals V of Glatter (1981). For a centro-symmetric scatterer the q-dependent part of C_i is exactly the rank-one matrix c_dim a(q_i) a(q_i)^T, which is what is used here (exact shell integrals, no p(r) discretisation error). Instrumental smearing turns C_i into a sum of such rank-one terms (see pysasfit.core.resolution).

The distance distribution p(r) = r^(dim-1) gamma(r) (eq. 4, 5) is still available from pddf() for output and comparison with ordinary IFT.

Instead of shells, a B-spline basis can be passed as grid (BSplineBasis); all functions here then use it (amplitudes by Gauss quadrature per knot span).

Regularisation functionals of Hansen (2016), eqs. (14) and (15).

Smoothness (eq. 14):

S = sum_j (f_j - (f_{j-1} + f_{j+1})/2)^2

End conditions for the density profile: at r = 0 the profile is continued symmetrically (f_{-1} = f_1, rho is an even function through the centre); at r = r_max the end point is either free (no row, Hansen’s “free end points”) or forced towards zero (f_N = 0).

Maximum entropy (eq. 15):

S = sum_j f_j ln(f_j / m_j) - f_j + m_j

with prior m (requires f > 0, i.e. a positive contrast profile).

pysasfit.sldprofile.regularization.smoothness_operator(n, outer='free', grid=None)[source]

Operator D with S = |D f|^2 (eq. 14). outer in {‘free’, ‘zero’}.
