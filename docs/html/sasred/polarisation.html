Polarised data without spin analysis (SANSPOL)

SANS-I can polarise the incident beam: the polariser (a supermirror cavity in the first collimation section) is in or out, the RF flipper before the sample is on or off. There is no analyser behind the sample. SANS-LLB has no polarisation option. Every state is measured and reduced as a run of its own (one HDF file each):

I+ and I− are the cross sections for a fully polarised beam with the neutron spin parallel (+) or antiparallel (−) to the field at the sample, summed over the final spin states (I± = I±± + I±∓). This is the convention of Moon, Riste & Koehler, Phys. Rev. 181, 920 (1969): the amplitudes are U±± = b ± p M⊥z, so a spin parallel to the magnetisation adds the magnetic amplitude, I++ = NN + MH + 2NM, I−− = NN + MH − 2NM, and I− − I+ = −4 NM (+ 4 CH in chiral samples) is the nuclear–magnetic interference; (I+ + I−)/2 is the unpolarised cross section.

Note

Michels, the SASfit C functions and part of the magnetic-SANS literature label the states by the neutron magnetic moment, which is antiparallel to the spin: their “++” is the “−−” here (Quan et al. 2019). pySASred and pySASstudio label by the spin. Which flipper state gives spin + is set in the instrument file (flipper_off_is_plus, default: flipper off = +, to be confirmed for SANS-I) and can be changed per data set; a sample with a known sign of N·M settles it (I− − I+ < 0 for N·M > 0).

Polariser and flipper state of a run

  • Polariser: stored in the raw file, entry1/SANS/polarizer/state (“in” / “out”; read as raw.polariser, empty for older files without the entry).

  • Flipper: not stored in the raw files. pySASred takes it

    1. from what was set by hand in the run table: right mouse button → Flipper state → flipper off / on, or alternate (off, on, off, … or on, off, … in run order, for the usual alternating measurement); kept per raw-data folder like the corrected sample names (names.py, also after Rescan);

    2. otherwise from the sample name: “flipper on”, “flip_off”, “flipper=on” or a trailing “_on” / “-off” / “ (on)” (a plain “on” inside a name, as in “PNIPAM on silica”, is not used).

    Runs with the polariser in and an unknown flipper state are shown in orange in the run table, and the reduction plan says so (“flipper off and on runs would otherwise be summed”).

The grouping keeps polariser in / out and flipper off / on apart (polariser and flipper are part of the default matching), the rows of polarised samples are labelled “name [flipper off]” / “name [flipper on]” (the transmission and the sample position are matched by the plain name), a row that sums runs of different states gets a warning, and the reduced files carry polariser, flipper and flipper_source in their metadata, from where the correction and pySASstudio take the state.

Efficiencies

symbol

meaning

relation

P

beam polarisation

p = (1 + P)/2 neutrons in the + state with flipper off; flipping ratio (1 + P)/(1 − P)

f

flipped fraction of the flipper

flipper “efficiency” F = 2f − 1 (Quan et al. quote F = 0.99, i.e. f = 0.995)

P_f

polarisation after the sample (depolarisation)

r = (1 − P_f)/(−ln P_f); P_f = 1: no depolarisation

With Pe = P r:

n+_off = (1 + Pe)/2,           n−_off = (1 − Pe)/2
n−_on  = f n+_off + (1 − f) n−_off,   n+_on = 1 − n−_on            (exact)

BerSANS (Keiderling et al. 2008, eq. 3b) uses n−_on = p f, which leaves out the (1 − p)(1 − f) term; the option form="bersans" reproduces data corrected that way.

Depolarisation (Quan et al., IUCrJ 8 (2021) 455): in a ferromagnet that is not saturated the beam polarisation decays exponentially along the path, P(x) = P exp(−D x). Neutrons scattered at depth x see the local polarisation, so for SANSPOL the incident polarisation is replaced by its depth average P r (P_f = 0.9 → r = 0.949, 0.75 → 0.869). P_f is measured in transmission, which needs an analyser; without it P_f = 1.

Where the values come from

  1. The metadata of the reduced file (header items of the raw run), with the keys listed in the instrument file under polarisation: metadata_keys (SANS-I: polariser / entry1/SANS/polarizer/state, flipper). P and f are not stored in the SANS-I files.

  2. Otherwise the instrument defaults (instruments/sinq/sans_i.yaml, section polarisation):

    • P(λ, collimation) from the table of Aswal et al., NIM A 586 (2008) 86, Fig. 4 (proton spin filter, δP/P ≤ 0.5 %), interpolated linearly in λ and in log(collimation). Outside the measured range the nearest value is used and the source says “extrapolated”. A collimation longer than 15 m is flagged: the polariser sits in the first collimation section and cannot be in.

Every value carries its source, which is written into the notes and the output files.

Important

Without an analyser P cannot be determined from the data: only the product P (I+ − I−) is measured. A wrong P scales I+ − I− by 1/P and leaves (I+ + I−)/2 unchanged. f, on the other hand, follows from the three states (flipped_fraction_from_data), provided the polariser-out data are on the same absolute scale.

Procedure

  1. Reduce every state as usual: same mask, binning (1D) or pixel grid (2D), background and empty cell measured in the same state, transmission, water or direct beam. The correction is linear and is applied to the fully corrected data, never to raw counts (Keiderling et al. 2008). Sample transmissions of the two flipper states agree unless the sample absorbs spin-dependently.

  2. Combine the states (two or all three):

    I+ − I− = (I_off − I_on) / (f Pe)
I_u     = I_off − (I_off − I_on) / (2 f)          (independent of P)
I±      = I_u ± (I+ − I−)/2

    With all three states I+ and I− follow per point from weighted least squares (weights 1/variance of each state); chi² per point (one degree of freedom, median ≈ 0.45 when consistent) checks P, f and the scale of the polariser-out data.

  3. Outputs: I+, I−, (I+ + I−)/2 and I− − I+, each as a reduced data set (2D image or 1D curve) with the union of the masks.

Uncertainties: every source of the reduced states is kept and propagated exactly to first order; sources shared by the states (the same background, Cd or water run) are counted once. P, f and P_f enter as systematic scalar sources (group polarisation) when their uncertainty is known (the SANS-I table: σ_P = 0.005); otherwise “uncertainty unknown, not propagated” is noted. For files the inputs carry only Idev, so the outputs have the statistical Idev; the covariance and systematic sensitivities of the inputs are removed from the output files because they belong to the uncorrected intensity.

For fitting a model it is better to fit the measured states directly with the forward model (sanspol_forward, pySASstudio polarisation box): no noise amplification by the inversion. The corrected I+, I− and I− − I+ are for display and model-free analysis.

Scripting

From reduced files (written by pySASred, 1D or 2D):

from pysasfit.sasred.polarisation import correct_files

out = correct_files({"off": "sample_off.h5", "on": "sample_on.h5",
                     "unpolarised": "sample_unpol.h5"},      # optional third state
                    out_prefix="sample_sanspol")
# -> sample_sanspol_plus.h5, _minus.h5, _unpolarised.h5, _difference.h5

P and f come from the metadata, else from the SANS-I defaults; give them explicitly with correct_files(..., P=0.975, f=0.99, P_f=0.9) or a full setup:

from pysasfit.sasred.polarisation import PolarisationSetup, sanspol_correct, correct_reduced

setup = PolarisationSetup(P=0.984, f=0.995, sigma_P=0.005, P_f=1.0,
                          form="exact", flipper_off_is_plus=True)
print(setup.describe())

# reduced results of one session (Reduced2D or ReducedRun): all sources stay correlated
res = correct_reduced(setup, off=r_off, on=r_on, unpolarised=r_unpol)
res["difference"].image          # UArray, I- - I+ per pixel
res["result"].chi2               # with three states

# arrays or UArrays directly
r = sanspol_correct({"off": I_off, "on": I_on}, setup)
r.plus, r.minus, r.unpolarised, r.difference

Default P of SANS-I for a setting:

from pysasfit.sasred.polarisation import beam_polarisation_table
P, sigma, source = beam_polarisation_table(wavelength_nm=0.6, collimation_m=6)   # 0.984, 0.005
