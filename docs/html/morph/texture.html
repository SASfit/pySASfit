pySASmorph: anisotropic data (texture)

pySASmorph also builds 3D structures from a 2D pattern of an oriented sample, when the sample is rotationally symmetric about an axis that lies in the detector plane (fibre or uniaxial texture). Typical examples:

  • stretched elastomers and drawn polymers;

  • extruded, flow-aligned or field-aligned samples;

  • compressed foams.

One 2D pattern then fixes the full 3D intensity, because the detector plane contains every angle to the axis.

File → Anisotropic 2D data (texture)… in pySASmorph opens the window. In Python the module is pysasfit.morph.anisotropic. The theory is in pySASmorph: theory and mathematics, section 12.

Data

Load one 2D pattern with the background subtracted, or fit the flat background B in the window:

  • NXcanSAS 2D, e.g. pySASred’s File → Save 2D NXcanSAS. The file has I, Idev, Qx, Qy and Mask; masked pixels are dropped.

  • .npz with qx, qy, I and optionally sigma, mask.

  • Text file with the columns q_x q_y I [dI].

Lengths are in the inverse unit of q. q_x, q_y and the axis angle follow the frame of pySASfit (Axis and angle conventions): q_x horizontal (to the right in the view towards the sample), q_y up, angles counter-clockwise from +q_x. pySASred 2D files written before 2026-11-23 (q_x of the other sign) are corrected when read.

Two models

A – affine stretch

B – uniaxial (Legendre)

any structure with uniaxial symmetry

λ and the axis angle, by collapsing log I onto one curve of q_eff = (λ²q∥² + q⊥²)^½

the Legendre components I_l(q), l = 0, 2, …, l_max

then

the collapsed curve goes through the isotropic pipeline; γ(r) = γ_iso((r∥²/λ² + r⊥²)^½)

γ(r) from the components by the plane-wave expansion

use when

the iso-intensity lines are ellipses

streaks, meridional peaks, any shape

Both then use the same steps:

  1. The level-cut inversion \(g = F^{-1}(\varphi(1-\varphi)\gamma)\), direction by direction.

  2. FFT synthesis of the Gaussian field from \(g(\mathbf r)\) on the periodic grid.

  3. The cut, the voxel scattering, and the statistics.

The symmetry axis

The 2D pattern is mirror-symmetric about two perpendicular lines. Which of them is the 3D symmetry axis is a choice: the 3D structure is rotated about it.

  • estimate sets the axis perpendicular to the direction of maximum intensity (the fibre convention). Fibres and stretched networks scatter an equatorial streak perpendicular to the axis.

  • +90° takes the other line, for layers along the axis (meridional peaks).

  • Model A refines the angle (refine the axis).

In the result the axis is the box x axis and the beam the box z axis.

Settings

  • l_max (B): the highest even Legendre order. Strong anisotropy needs more orders, noisy data fewer; 6–8 is typical.

  • Coverage: q bins without full azimuthal coverage are left out and listed in the warnings. These are the bins at the beam stop and in the detector corners.

  • volume fraction φ, box size L, voxels n, seed: as in the isotropic case. L defaults to 5·2π/q* of the Kratky maximum of I_0; in model A it is enlarged by λ.

  • q range, low-q extrapolation, Porod range, background: as in the isotropic case. The Porod range is automatic by default (or the last N points, q ≥ q_min, the last 15 %). fit flat (incoherent) background B is on by default; untick it to use the fixed B (empty = 0). They apply to I_0 (model B) or to the collapsed curve (model A).

Results

the report

The report lists:

  • the model;

  • the axis and λ;

  • Q_0, K_0, the contrast and S/V (Porod);

  • clipped γ values;

  • χ² of the level-cut model against all pixels;

  • S/V of the voxels;

  • the share of interface faces normal to x / y / z, each 1/3 when isotropic;

  • the mean chord lengths of both phases along and across the axis;

  • clusters and percolation per direction.

For a structure stretched by λ, the chords along the axis are λ times those across it.

File → Export components and maps… writes four files: the data and model components (text), γ and g on the (r, μ) table (.npz), and the report. Export voxel matrix… writes VTK, TIFF or npy.

from pysasfit.morph.anisotropic import AnisoParameters, load_2d, reconstruct_aniso

d = load_2d("stretched_2d.h5")
res = reconstruct_aniso(d, AnisoParameters(mode="B", axis_deg=None, porosity=0.3, n=128, lmax=8))
print(res.report())           # axis None: estimated (fibre convention), noted in the warnings

Limits

  • Uniaxial only. A general texture without a symmetry axis needs several sample orientations, as in SAXS tensor tomography (to come).

  • Not for locally varying orientations. A sample made of differently oriented domains, like a polycrystal, is not one stationary Gaussian field and is outside the model.

  • Axis in the detector plane. An axis tilted out of the plane leaves part of the μ range unmeasured.

  • Realisability. As in the isotropic case, γ values a level-cut field cannot reach are clipped and counted. Negative spectral power of the anisotropic g is reported.

Validation (tests/morph/test_anisotropic.py)

The tests use exact synthetic data: a level-cut structure with a Gaussian g, stretched by λ = 2 along 30°, with 1 % noise. They check:

  • Model A: it recovers λ to 0.2 % and the axis to 0.2°.

  • γ(r∥, r⊥): models A and B both reproduce it within 0.025 at six points along, across and between the axes.

  • Fit quality: the contrast is right within 3 % and χ² stays below 20 (it is about 4–12).

  • Isotropic data: they give no higher components, I_l/I_0 < 2 %.

  • Reconstruction: the voxel porosity is exact; the chords along the axis are λ times those across; fewer interface faces are normal to the axis. Voxel and Porod S/V agree, and less than 2 % negative power is discarded.

  • Constants: the tail integrals ∫j_l/x² dx have their closed forms.

  • Files and window: loading NXcanSAS, npz and text files; the window, opened from pySASmorph’s File menu.
