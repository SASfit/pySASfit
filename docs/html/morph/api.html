pySASmorph: API reference

Generated from the docstrings.

The SASmorph chain: I(q) -> gamma(r) -> g(r) -> f(k) -> clipped Gaussian random field.

from pysasfit.core import load_data from pysasfit.morph import MorphParameters, reconstruct

q, I, s, _ = load_data(“sample.dat”) res = reconstruct(q, I, s, MorphParameters(porosity=0.3, box_size=2000, n=128)) res.matrix # bool (n, n, n), True = phase 2 (“pore”) res.statistics # S/V, connectivity, bicontinuity print(res.report())

Steps (Ingham et al., J. Appl. Cryst. 44 (2011) 221; Berk, Phys. Rev. A 44 (1991) 5069; Quintanilla, Modelling Simul. Mater. Sci. Eng. 15 (2007) S337):

  1. extrapolate I(q) to q = 0 and (Porod) to infinity, invariant Q

  2. gamma(r) = (1/Q) integral q^2 I(q) sin(qr)/(qr) dq

  3. g(r) from phi (1-phi) gamma(r) = F_alpha(g(r)) (level-cut relation)

  4. spectral function f(k) (3D Fourier transform of g)

  5. Gaussian random field with correlation g on an n^3 grid, cut at alpha

  6. statistics and the back-calculated scattering of the structure

The reconstruction is a morphology with the measured two-point statistics, not the morphology: it is meaningful for isotropic, non-periodic two-phase samples whose data reach the Porod regime.

pysasfit.morph.pipeline.porosity_from_invariant(Q, delta_rho, q_unit='A')[source]

Volume fraction (the root <= 0.5) from an absolute invariant.

Q in cm^-1 * (q unit)^-3 with q in 1/Angstrom (q_unit='A') or 1/nm (‘nm’), delta_rho the scattering length density difference in cm^-2.

Multi-phase SASmorph: one Gaussian field cut at several levels, one or several contrasts.

Model

A unit-variance Gaussian field psi(r) with correlation g(r) is cut at the levels alpha_1 < … < alpha_{N-1}; phase i occupies alpha_i < psi < alpha_{i+1} (alpha_0 = -inf, alpha_N = +inf), so the phases are ordered: phase i touches only phases i-1 and i+1, and a middle phase forms a layer (film, shell) between its neighbours. The volume fractions fix the levels, alpha_k = Phi^-1(phi_0 + … + phi_{k-1}).

With the scattering length density rho_i of phase i, rho(psi) = rho_0 + sum_k D_k [psi > alpha_k] with the jumps D_k = rho_k - rho_{k-1}. By Plackett’s identity for the bivariate normal distribution the SLD correlation is

C(r) = <drho(0) drho(r)> = sum_kl D_k D_l B(alpha_k, alpha_l; g(r)), B(a, b; g) = 1/(2 pi) integral_0^g exp(-(a^2 + b^2 - 2 t a b) / (2 (1 - t^2))) / sqrt(1 - t^2) dt

(Berk 1991; Roberts 1997 for the two-cut phase; Teubner 1991). One cut gives the two-phase relation of pysasfit.morph.correlation. C(0) = sum phi_i rho_i^2 - (sum phi_i rho_i)^2.

Data

Every curve m (a contrast: SANS contrast variation, SAXS + SANS, …) has its own SLD set and gives gamma_m(r) = C_m(r)/C_m(0). The common g(r) minimises, at each r,

sum_m w_m (gamma_m(r) - G_m(g))^2, G_m(g) = C_m(g) / C_m(0),

found on a fine g grid (robust also where G_m is not monotonic for g < 0). With several contrasts the volume fractions can be fitted as well (fit_fractions): the profile misfit over r is minimised. With SLDs in absolute units (1e-6 / A^2 = 1e10 cm^-2) and data on absolute scale, the invariants Q_m = 2 pi^2 C_m(0) are a check of fractions, SLDs and calibration, and can enter the fraction fit.

SESANS in pySASmorph: the projected correlation function and its inversion.

Spin-echo SANS measures (Andersson et al., J. Appl. Cryst. 41 (2008) 868)

G(delta) - G(0) = 1/(2 pi) integral I(Q) [J0(Q delta) - 1] Q dQ

= integral [C(sqrt(delta^2 + x^2)) - C(|x|)] dx,

with C(r) the correlation function of the scattering length density in the units of the data (I(Q) = 4 pi integral C(r) r^2 sin(Qr)/(Qr) dr; I in 1/cm, lengths in the unit of the file: G in [length]^-2 cm^-1, as pysasfit.core.sesans_io.load_sesans() returns it). The real-space projection is the Abel transform

integral C(sqrt(delta^2 + x^2)) dx = 2 integral_delta^inf C(r) r / sqrt(r^2 - delta^2) dr.

projection_matrix() evaluates it exactly for C piecewise linear on an r grid (the integrals of (a + b r) r / sqrt(r^2 - delta^2) are elementary, the square-root singularity is handled analytically). invert_sesans() solves G = A C for C on an r grid with a second- difference (Tikhonov) penalty on nodes that follow the spacing of the data, the regularisation chosen by the discrepancy principle (generalised cross-validation without errors); the normalised gamma(r) = C(r)/C(0) of a SESANS curve then enters the joint fit of g(r) of pysasfit.morph.multiphase like the gamma of a scattering curve, on the r range the spin-echo lengths cover. The forward model of a SESANS curve is the projection of the level-cut covariance C(g(r)), with one scale factor fitted.

For a periodic voxel structure voxel_correlation() gives C(r) from the autocorrelation of the SLD map (radial average, r < L/2).

Extrapolation of I(q) to q = 0 and q = infinity, and the scattering invariant.

The correlation function needs the intensity over the whole q axis. As in SAXSMorph (Ingham et al. 2011) the measured range is extended

  • to q -> 0 by a Guinier, linear or constant extrapolation fitted to the first points (low_q), and

  • to q -> infinity by Porod’s law I = K q^-4 fitted to the last points, optionally together with a flat background B (I = K q^-4 + B; B is then subtracted from all data).

Start of the Porod range chosen from the data.

For every candidate start q_i (from start_fraction of the points on, at least min_points points left) the law I = K q^-4 (+ B) is fitted to q >= q_i, with its reduced chi^2 (relative scatter when no sigma is given) and the lag-1 autocorrelation rho of its residuals. A candidate is acceptable when its chi^2 is at most tolerance times the smallest chi^2 of all candidates, or at most chi2_ok (with sigma), and its residuals show no trend (rho below 3/sqrt(N), at least 0.15: a range reaching into the non-Porod region leaves residuals of one sign, even when generous error bars keep chi^2 small). Two-point statistics of the clipped (level-cut) Gaussian random field.

A Gaussian random field psi(r) with unit variance and correlation function g(r) = <psi(0) psi(r)>, g(0) = 1, is cut at the level alpha: phase 2 (“pore”) is psi > alpha, so its volume fraction is phi = P(psi > alpha) = erfc(alpha/sqrt 2)/2. The indicator correlation of the cut field (Berk 1987/1991; Quintanilla 2007) is:

S2(r) - phi^2 = F(g(r)),
F(g) = 1/(2 pi) integral_0^g exp(-alpha^2/(1+t)) / sqrt(1-t^2) dt
     = 1/(2 pi) integral_0^{arcsin g} exp(-alpha^2/(1+sin theta)) d theta,

with F(1) = phi(1-phi). For a two-phase sample the scattering gives S2(r) - phi^2 = phi(1-phi) gamma(r) (Debye), hence:

g(r) = F^-1( phi(1-phi) gamma(r) ).

F is tabulated in theta (smooth integrand, no endpoint singularity) and inverted by monotone interpolation. Values of gamma outside the range a level-cut field can reach are clipped and counted (LevelCut.clipped).

The spectral function is the 3D Fourier transform of g:

f(k) = 4 pi integral r^2 g(r) sin(kr)/(kr) dr,

and the distribution of wave-vector magnitudes of the field is P(k) = k^2 f(k) / (2 pi^2) (integral P dk = g(0) = 1).

Exact sine transforms of piecewise-linear functions (Filon-type quadrature).

All isotropic Fourier transforms in pysasfit.morph reduce to

T(r) = integral_{x_0}^{x_N} u(x) sin(x r) dx

with u given on nodes x_0 < ... < x_N and linear in between. The integral is evaluated exactly for that interpolant, so it stays accurate for any r: the quadrature does not need many points per oscillation (a plain trapezoid rule on the data grid fails as soon as r * dq ~ 1, which is where SAXSMorph’s high-frequency ripple in Gamma(r) and f(k) comes from).

For small r * x_N the closed form suffers from cancellation; there the Taylor series in r with exact moments of the interpolant is used instead.

The Porod tail u(q) = K q^-3 beyond q_N (i.e. I(q) = K q^-4) is integrated analytically with the sine integral, see porod_tail().

SASmorph for anisotropic samples with a symmetry axis in the detector plane (texture).

A 2D pattern I(q_x, q_y) (beam along z, small angles: q_z = 0) of a sample with uniaxial symmetry about an axis e in the detector plane, at the angle axis_deg from q_x towards q_y, determines the full 3D intensity: I(q) depends only on q (the length of the scattering vector) and mu, the absolute cosine of its angle to e (centrosymmetry folds mu to [0, 1]), and the detector plane contains every mu. Two models:

mode="B" – uniaxial, any shape of the anisotropy

I(q, mu) = sum_l I_l(q) P_l(mu) (even l <= lmax) by least squares in q bins; each I_l(q) is extended to q = 0 and to infinity (l = 0 as the isotropic curve, l >= 2 with I_l ~ q^2 at low q and I_l = K_l q^-4 beyond the data); the 3D correlation function follows from the plane-wave expansion

C(r) = sum_l C_l(r) P_l(r^.e), C_l(r) = (-1)^(l/2) / (2 pi^2) integral q^2 I_l(q) j_l(qr) dq.

mode="A" – affine: an isotropic structure stretched by lambda along the axis (both perpendicular

directions unchanged): I(q) = lambda I_iso(sqrt(lambda^2 q_par^2 + q_perp^2)). lambda (and the axis, refined from the start value) is fitted by collapsing the 2D data onto one curve; that curve goes through the isotropic pipeline, and gamma(r) = gamma_iso(sqrt(r_par^2/lambda^2 + r_perp^2)).

Either way the level-cut relation S_2(r) - phi^2 = F(g(r)) holds for every vector r, so g(r) = F^-1(phi (1 - phi) gamma(r)) direction by direction; the Gaussian field is synthesised by FFT from g sampled on the periodic grid (as pysasfit.morph.grf.field_fft(), without assuming that g depends on the distance only).

Box frame: the symmetry axis is the box x axis (array axis 0), the beam the box z axis (array axis 2); the detector plane is the box (x, y) plane with q_par = q_x(box), q_perp = q_y(box).

Checks: the level-cut model I_l(q) (ensemble average with the reconstructed g) and the voxel I_l(q) (all 3D wave vectors of the voxel structure) against the data components, and the 2D maps.

Direction of maximum intensity from the cos 2phi moment of the pattern (each q shell normalised to its mean): (phi_max in degrees, anisotropy 0..1). The pattern is mirror-symmetric about phi_max and phi_max + 90 deg; which of them is the 3D symmetry axis is a choice: fibres (equatorial streak) have their axis at phi_max + 90, layers along the axis (meridional peaks) at phi_max.
