pySASmorph: theory and mathematics

This page derives every step of pySASmorph in the order the program runs. Each step names the function that implements it. Lengths and q are in the units of the data, e.g. Å and Å⁻¹. Intensities are in the units of the data, so absolute values need data on an absolute scale.

1. Two-phase structures and their scattering

A two-phase sample has the scattering length density (SLD)

\[\begin{split} \eta(\mathbf r) = \rho_1 + \Delta\rho\, \chi(\mathbf r), \qquad \chi(\mathbf r) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{phase 2 ("pore")}\\ 0 & \text{phase 1 ("solid")} \end{cases} \end{split}\]

with the volume fraction \(\varphi = \langle\chi\rangle\) of phase 2 and the contrast \(\Delta\rho = \rho_2-\rho_1\).

Two-point function. The two-point probability \(S_2(r) = \langle \chi(\mathbf 0)\chi(\mathbf r)\rangle\) of an isotropic sample falls from \(S_2(0) = \varphi\) to \(S_2(\infty) = \varphi^2\). The normalised correlation function of Debye and Bueche (1949) is

\[ \gamma(r) = \frac{\langle \delta\eta(\mathbf 0)\,\delta\eta(\mathbf r)\rangle}{\langle\delta\eta^2\rangle} = \frac{S_2(r)-\varphi^2}{\varphi(1-\varphi)}, \qquad \gamma(0) = 1 . \]

Scattering. The macroscopic cross-section is its Fourier transform:

\[ I(q) = 4\pi\,\Delta\rho^2\,\varphi(1-\varphi) \int_0^\infty r^2\,\gamma(r)\,\frac{\sin qr}{qr}\,\mathrm dr . \]

The inverse transform gives \(\gamma\) from the data:

\[ \gamma(r) = \frac{1}{Q}\int_0^\infty q^2 I(q)\,\frac{\sin qr}{qr}\,\mathrm dq . \]

Here \(Q\) is the invariant (Porod 1951):

\[ Q = \int_0^\infty q^2 I(q)\,\mathrm dq = 2\pi^2\,\Delta\rho^2\,\varphi(1-\varphi) . \]

Sharp interfaces. A sharp interface of area \(S\) per volume \(V\) gives Porod’s law. It also fixes the slope of \(\gamma\) at the origin (Debye, Anderson and Brumberger 1957):

\[ I(q) \xrightarrow{q\to\infty} \frac{K}{q^4}, \quad K = 2\pi\,\Delta\rho^2\,\frac SV, \qquad\qquad \gamma(r) \simeq 1 - \frac{S/V}{4\varphi(1-\varphi)}\,r \quad (r\to 0) . \]

Dividing \(K\) by \(Q\) removes the contrast:

\[ \frac SV = \pi\,\varphi(1-\varphi)\,\frac KQ \qquad\text{({func}`~pysasfit.morph.statistics.specific_surface_porod`).} \]

Contrast from the invariant. For a known \(\varphi\) the invariant gives the contrast in the units of the data (contrast_from_invariant()):

\[ \Delta\rho^2 = Q / \bigl(2\pi^2\varphi(1-\varphi)\bigr) . \]

All back-calculated curves use this contrast, so they are on the scale of the data. Conversely, with absolute data (\(I\) in cm⁻¹) and a known \(\Delta\rho\) in cm⁻², \(\varphi(1-\varphi) = Q/(2\pi^2\Delta\rho^2)\) gives the volume fraction. The program takes the root \(\le \tfrac12\) (porosity_from_invariant()). \(Q\) is converted with 10²⁴ for q in Å⁻¹ and 10²¹ for q in nm⁻¹.

2. Extending the data to q = 0 and q = ∞

The transforms need \(I(q)\) on the whole q axis (extrapolate()).

Background and Porod law. The Porod law, with an optional flat background \(B\), is fitted to the Porod range. The fit is linear in \(q^4\), by weighted least squares with the weights \(w = 1/(\sigma q^4)\):

\[ I\,q^4 = K + B\,q^4 . \]

\(B\) is then subtracted from all data. \(B\) can also be fixed by hand, and then it is subtracted before \(K\) is fitted. The parameter uncertainties are the least-squares covariance, scaled by \(\chi^2_\text{red}\) when that is larger than 1.

The Porod range. It is given by hand (the last N points, or \(q \ge q_\text{min}\)) or chosen automatically (auto_porod_range()); the former default was the last 15 %, at least 5 points. The automatic choice works in three steps:

  1. Candidates. Every start \(q_i\) from 40 % of the points on (up to 60 candidates, at least 6 points) is fitted. With \(\varepsilon_j = (I_j - Kq_j^{-4} - B)/\sigma_j\) (relative residuals without errors) it gets

    \[ \chi^2_i = \frac{1}{N_i-p}\sum_{j\ge i}\varepsilon_j^2, \qquad \rho_i = \frac{\sum_j \delta_j\,\delta_{j+1}}{\sum_j\delta_j^2},\quad \delta_j = \varepsilon_j - \bar\varepsilon . \]

    \(\rho_i\) is the lag-1 autocorrelation of the residuals.

  2. Acceptance. A start is acceptable when two conditions hold:

    • \(\chi^2_i \le 1.5\,\min_k\chi^2_k\), or \(\chi^2_i \le 2\) with error bars;

    • \(\rho_i < \max(0.15,\ 3/\sqrt{N_i})\), i.e. no trend beyond 3σ of white noise.

    A range reaching into the non-Porod region leaves residuals of one sign even when generous error bars keep \(\chi^2\) small. The lowest acceptable \(q_i\) gives the longest range and so the most precise \(K\) and \(B\); if no start is acceptable, the best fit is taken.

  3. No clear Porod regime. The report warns in three cases:

    • the best \(\chi^2 > 6\) with error bars;

    • the range spans less than a factor 1.5 in q, so \(K\) and \(B\) cannot be separated;

    • \(Kq_i^{-4} < |B|\) at the start, so the “fit” is a constant.

A fitted \(B\) also absorbs part of the next term of the Porod expansion (e.g. \(\propto q^{-6}\)) near the start of the range.

Low q. The first points (default 10) give the extrapolation from q = 0 to \(q_\text{min}\), on 40 nodes (fit_low_q()).

\[ u(q) = q\,I(q) \]

is treated as piecewise linear. Beyond \(q_N\) the Porod law \(K q^{-4}\) holds up to infinity.

Invariant. The invariant is exact for this interpolant, with the Porod tail integrated analytically (invariant):

\[ Q = \sum_i \int_{q_i}^{q_{i+1}} q\,u(q)\,\mathrm dq + \frac{K}{q_N} . \]

The program reports the share \(K/(q_N Q)\) of the Porod tail. Above 20 % the q range is probably too short.

3. Exact sine transforms

Every isotropic transform in pySASmorph has the form \(T(r) = \int_{x_0}^{x_N} u(x)\sin(xr)\,\mathrm dx\) with \(u\) piecewise linear (sine_transform_pl()). For example, \(Q\gamma(r) = T(r)/r\) with \(u = qI\).

Exact formula. On a segment \([a,b]\) with slope \(s\):

\[ \int_a^b u(x)\sin(xr)\,\mathrm dx = \frac{u_a\cos ar - u_b\cos br}{r} + s\,\frac{\sin br - \sin ar}{r^2} . \]

Summed over the segments, the first term telescopes to \(\bigl(u_0\cos x_0 r - u_N\cos x_N r\bigr)/r\). This Filon-type quadrature is exact for the interpolant at any \(r\). It needs no points per oscillation. A trapezoid rule on the data grid breaks down once \(r\,\Delta q \sim 1\); that is where the ripple of SAXSMorph’s \(\Gamma(r)\) and \(f(k)\) comes from.

Small r. For \(r\,x_N < 0.05\) the closed form loses digits by cancellation. The program then uses the Taylor series with the exact moments \(m_n = \int x^n u\,\mathrm dx\) of the interpolant (moments_pl()):

\[ T(r) = r\,m_1 - \frac{r^3 m_3}{3!} + \frac{r^5 m_5}{5!} - \frac{r^7 m_7}{7!} . \]

Porod tail. The tail \(I = Kq^{-4}\) for \(q>q_N\) contributes, with the sine integral \(\mathrm{Si}\) (porod_tail()):

\[ \int_{q_N}^\infty q^2\,\frac{K}{q^4}\,\frac{\sin qr}{qr}\,\mathrm dq = K\,r\,J_3(q_N r), \qquad J_3(a) = \int_a^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x^3}\,\mathrm dx = \frac{\sin a}{2a^2} + \frac{\cos a}{2a} - \frac12\Bigl(\frac\pi2 - \mathrm{Si}(a)\Bigr) . \]

Its limit at \(r = 0\) is \(K/q_N\), the Porod part of the invariant.

4. The clipped Gaussian random field

Gaussian random field. \(\psi(\mathbf r)\) is a statistically homogeneous and isotropic Gaussian random field with zero mean, unit variance and correlation function

\[ g(r) = \langle\psi(\mathbf 0)\,\psi(\mathbf r)\rangle, \qquad g(0) = 1 . \]

Level cut. Cutting it at the level \(\alpha\) gives a two-phase structure (Cahn 1965; Berk 1987, 1991):

\[ \chi(\mathbf r) = H\bigl(\psi(\mathbf r)-\alpha\bigr), \qquad \varphi = P(\psi>\alpha) = \tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}\bigl(\alpha/\sqrt2\bigr),\quad \alpha = \sqrt2\,\operatorname{erfc}^{-1}(2\varphi) . \]

Two-point function of the cut field. \(\psi(\mathbf 0)\) and \(\psi(\mathbf r)\) are bivariate normal with correlation \(g\). With the bivariate normal density \(\phi_2\), Plackett’s identity (1954) states

\[ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,P\bigl(\psi_1>a,\ \psi_2>b\bigr)\Big|_{\operatorname{corr}=t} = \phi_2(a,b;t), \qquad \phi_2(a,b;t) = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{1-t^2}}\exp\!\Bigl[-\frac{a^2+b^2-2tab}{2(1-t^2)}\Bigr] . \]

At \(t = 0\) the two values are independent and \(S_2 = \varphi^2\). Integrating from there gives

\[ S_2(r)-\varphi^2 = F\bigl(g(r)\bigr),\qquad F(g) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{g}\frac{e^{-\alpha^2/(1+t)}}{\sqrt{1-t^2}}\,\mathrm dt = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^{\arcsin g} e^{-\alpha^2/(1+\sin\theta)}\,\mathrm d\theta . \]

Implementation. \(F\) is tabulated in \(\theta = \arcsin g\) on 20 001 points, where the integrand has no end-point singularity, and integrated by cumulative Simpson (LevelCut).

End values. They are the exact orthant probabilities. At \(g = 1\) the two points coincide. At \(g = -1\) \(\psi(\mathbf r) = -\psi(\mathbf 0)\), so both values exceed \(\alpha\) only between \(\alpha\) and \(-\alpha\):

\[ F(1) = \varphi(1-\varphi), \qquad F(-1) = -\min(\varphi,1-\varphi)^2, \qquad F(g) = \frac{\arcsin g}{2\pi}\ \ (\varphi=\tfrac12). \]

Inversion. Setting the model equal to the data, \(S_2-\varphi^2 = \varphi(1-\varphi)\gamma\), gives the field correlation:

\[ g(r) = F^{-1}\bigl(\varphi(1-\varphi)\,\gamma(r)\bigr) . \]

\(F\) is monotonic. It is inverted by interpolation in the table (θ as a function of F), then \(g = \sin\theta\). Values of \(\varphi(1-\varphi)\gamma\) outside \([F(-1), F(1)]\) cannot come from any level-cut field. They are clipped and counted (clipped). Many clipped points mean a wrong \(\varphi\), a wrong background, or a structure that is not a level cut.

Interfaces of the cut field. Near \(g = 1\) the integrand is \(\simeq e^{-\alpha^2/2}/\sqrt{2(1-t)}\), so

\[ F(1)-F(g) \simeq \frac{e^{-\alpha^2/2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{2(1-g)} . \]

A smooth field has \(g \simeq 1 + \tfrac12 g''(0)\,r^2\). Then \(1-\gamma\) is linear in \(r\), which is Porod behaviour. Comparison with §1 gives the interface area of the level surfaces:

\[ \frac SV = \frac2\pi\,\sqrt{-g''(0)}\;e^{-\alpha^2/2} . \]

This is twice Rice’s (1944) number of level crossings per unit length; the factor 2 is the stereological relation \(S/V = 2P_L\). Data that fall off more slowly than \(q^{-4}\) (rough or fractal interfaces) need a non-smooth \(g\) and lie outside the model.

5. Spectral function

The field is built from its spectral function, the 3D Fourier transform of \(g\) (spectral_function()):

\[ f(k) = 4\pi\int_0^\infty r^2\,g(r)\,\frac{\sin kr}{kr}\,\mathrm dr, \qquad g(r) = \frac{1}{2\pi^2}\int_0^\infty k^2 f(k)\,\frac{\sin kr}{kr}\,\mathrm dk . \]

It is computed with the transform of §3, with \(u = r\,g\).

Wave-vector distribution. Its distribution of magnitudes is normalised by \(g(0) = 1\) (wavevector_distribution()):

\[ P(k) = \frac{k^2 f(k)}{2\pi^2}, \qquad \int_0^\infty P(k)\,\mathrm dk = 1 . \]

Negative values of \(P\) are set to zero. A valid correlation function has \(f \ge 0\) (Bochner), so negative values signal an inconsistent \(g\).

r grid. \(g\) is evaluated on a uniform grid (pipeline._r_grid):

  • Range: from 0 to \(r_\text{max} = 1.02\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}L\), half the box diagonal.

  • Step: \(\Delta r = \min\bigl(a/4,\ \pi/(16\,q_\text{max})\bigr)\), so that the model \(I(q)\) is accurate up to \(q_\text{max}\).

A warning is given when \(|\gamma(r_\text{max})| > 0.05\), i.e. when the correlations extend beyond the box.

6. Generating the field

The box has edge \(L\) with \(n^3\) voxels of size \(a = L/n\) (pysasfit.morph.grf).

FFT spectral synthesis (default)

field_fft() follows the circulant-embedding idea (Dietrich and Newsam 1997):

  1. Sample \(g\) on the grid. \(g\) is sampled at the minimum-image distance of every voxel to the origin. This periodises \(g\) on the torus of edge \(L\); beyond \(r_\text{max}\), \(g = 0\).

  2. Power spectrum. \(S(\mathbf k) = \operatorname{Re}\,\mathrm{DFT}[g]\) on exactly the grid wave vectors \(\mathbf k = 2\pi\mathbf m/L\). Negative values, which come from a truncated or inconsistent \(g\), are set to zero. Their share of \(\sum|S|\) is reported as negative_power.

  3. Field. With white Gaussian noise \(w\) on the grid:

    \[ \psi = \mathrm{IFFT}\bigl[\sqrt{S}\;\mathrm{FFT}(w)\bigr], \]

    then shifted to mean 0 and scaled to variance 1.

The covariance of \(\psi\) on the grid is then exactly the periodised \(g\), apart from the discarded negative power. The cost is \(O(n^3\log n)\). The field is periodic, so \(L\) must span several correlation lengths.

Random waves (Berk; SAXSMorph)

field_cosine(), with method="cosine":

\[ \psi(\mathbf r) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{N_w}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_w}\cos(\mathbf k_i\cdot\mathbf r + \phi_i) . \]

  • \(|\mathbf k_i|\) is drawn from \(P(k)\) by inverse CDF. SAXSMorph uses rejection sampling.

  • The directions are uniform on the sphere and the phases \(\phi_i\) uniform in \([0, 2\pi)\).

For \(N_w\to\infty\) the field is Gaussian with correlation \(g\). It is not periodic. The cost is \(O(N_w n^3)\). The share of \(|\mathbf k_i| > \pi/a\), which the grid cannot represent, is reported.

The cut

level_cut() has two modes:

  • Quantile (default): the cut is at the empirical \((1-\varphi)\)-quantile of the voxel values, so the voxel porosity equals \(\varphi\) exactly.

  • α: the cut is at the theoretical level \(\alpha\), so the voxel porosity fluctuates around \(\varphi\).

7. Checks against the data

Level-cut model (levelcut_intensity()). This is the ensemble average of the model with the reconstructed \(g\). It equals the data wherever \(\gamma\) could be inverted without clipping:

\[ I_\text{lc}(q) = \Delta\rho^2\,4\pi\int_0^{r_\text{max}} r^2\,F\bigl(g(r)\bigr)\,\frac{\sin qr}{qr}\,\mathrm dr . \]

With measurement errors the report gives \(\chi^2 = \frac1N\sum\bigl[(I_\text{lc}-I)/\sigma\bigr]^2\) over the data points used (mean square, not divided by degrees of freedom).

Voxel structure (voxel_intensity()). This is what one realisation scatters:

\[ I_\text{vox}(q) = \Delta\rho^2\,\frac{a^3}{n^3}\,\Bigl\langle\bigl|\mathrm{DFT}[\chi-\bar\chi]\bigr|^2\Bigr\rangle_{|\mathbf k|\approx q} . \]

It is averaged in shells of width \(2\pi/L\); the half-space rfft planes are weighted twice. Expect speckle at low q, from the few wave vectors per shell, and voxel effects near \(\pi/a\).

8. Morphology of the voxel structure

pysasfit.morph.statistics and interface_areas() compute:

  • Interface area.

    \[ \frac SV = \frac23\,\frac{N_\text{faces}\,a^2}{n^3a^3}, \]

    with \(N_\text{faces}\) the voxel faces between unlike neighbours (periodic box). A staircase surface overestimates the area by the mean of \(|n_x|+|n_y|+|n_z|\) over isotropic normals, which is \(3/2\); hence the factor \(2/3\). Compare with \(S/V\) from Porod (§1).

  • Clusters. Connected components of each phase with 6-connectivity, the largest cluster’s share, and their number.

  • Percolation along x, y and z: a cluster touches both opposite faces of the box.

  • Bicontinuous (as SAXSMorph): each phase has a cluster that percolates in all three directions.

  • Degree of connectivity (as SAXSMorph): \((N_\text{largest pore}+N_\text{largest solid})/n^3\).

9. Several phases: one field, several cuts

Ordered phases

pysasfit.morph.multiphase. The same field is cut at \(N-1\) levels \(\alpha_1<\dots<\alpha_{N-1}\). Phase \(i\) occupies \(\alpha_i<\psi<\alpha_{i+1}\), with \(\alpha_0 = -\infty\) and \(\alpha_N = \infty\). The phases are therefore ordered: phase \(i\) touches only \(i\pm1\). A middle phase is a layer between its neighbours, e.g. a film, a shell or a wetting layer. The fractions fix the levels, with \(\Phi\) the standard normal distribution function:

\[ \alpha_k = \Phi^{-1}\Bigl(\sum_{i<k}\varphi_i\Bigr) . \]

SLD correlation

With the SLD jumps \(D_k = \rho_k-\rho_{k-1}\) the SLD is

\[ \rho(\psi) = \rho_0 + \sum_k D_k\,H(\psi-\alpha_k) . \]

Plackett’s identity (§4) applied to every pair of cuts gives the SLD correlation:

\[ C(r) = \langle\delta\rho(\mathbf 0)\,\delta\rho(\mathbf r)\rangle = \sum_{k,l} D_k D_l\, B\bigl(\alpha_k,\alpha_l;g(r)\bigr),\qquad B(a,b;g) = \int_0^g \phi_2(a,b;t)\,\mathrm dt . \]

It starts from

\[ C(0) = \sum_i\varphi_i\rho_i^2 - \Bigl(\sum_i\varphi_i\rho_i\Bigr)^2 . \]

  • Special cases: one cut reduces to §4; a single phase between two cuts is the two-cut model of Berk (1991), Roberts (1997) and Arleth et al. (2001).

  • Implementation: \(B\) is tabulated in \(\theta = \arcsin t\), like \(F\).

  • Invariant: \(Q_m = s\cdot 2\pi^2 C_m(0)\). The unit factor \(s\) is \(10^{-4}\) (q in Å⁻¹) or \(10^{-1}\) (q in nm⁻¹), for SLDs in 10⁻⁶ Å⁻² and \(I\) in cm⁻¹.

Several contrasts

Curve \(m\) has its own SLDs and gives

\[ \gamma_m(r) = C_m(r)/C_m(0) \quad\text{(data)},\qquad G_m(g) = C_m(g)/C_m(0) \quad\text{(model)} . \]

Common field correlation. At every \(r\) it minimises the weighted misfit (joint_g()):

\[ g(r) = \arg\min_g \sum_m w_m\bigl[\gamma_m(r) - G_m(g)\bigr]^2 . \]

The minimum is searched on a fine \(g\) grid, so it works where \(G_m\) is not monotonic.

Rescaling. For two or more curves each \(\gamma_m\) may be rescaled by \(\lambda_m\). This allows for an invariant that is a few per cent off when a curve does not reach its Porod regime:

\[ \lambda_m = \frac{\sum_r G_m(g)\,\gamma_m}{\sum_r\gamma_m^2} . \]

\(\lambda_m\) is clipped to \([0.8, 1.25]\), and \(g\) and \(\lambda\) are iterated. Corrections above 10 % are reported.

Fitting the fractions (fit_fractions()):

  • Parametrisation: \(\varphi_i = e^{x_i}/\sum e^{x_j}\) with \(x_0 = 0\), minimised by Nelder–Mead.

  • Objective: the profile misfit \(\sum_m w_m\,\overline{(\gamma_m - G_m)^2}\), plus \(10^{-2}\sum_m w_m \bigl[\ln\frac{Q_m}{2\pi^2C_m(0)} - c\bigr]^2\).

  • Invariant offset \(c\): the weighted mean of the logarithms when all curves share one intensity scale (only the ratios count), or \(\ln s\) for absolute data.

  • Weight: with \(10^{-2}\), a 10 % invariant mismatch counts as an rms \(\gamma\) misfit of 0.01.

  • Accuracy: the shapes alone fix the fractions only weakly, to a few hundredths in the tests.

Interfaces between phases. \(S_{ij}/V\) is found by the face count of §8, for every pair of phases \(i<j\). Non-neighbouring phases touch only where a layer is thinner than a voxel.

SESANS curves

Spin-echo SANS (Rekveldt 1996; Andersson et al. 2008) measures the projection of the same SLD correlation function \(C(r)\) (with \(I(Q) = 4\pi\int r^2 C(r)\,\frac{\sin Qr}{Qr}\,\mathrm dr\)) along the beam:

\[ G(\delta) - G(0) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty I(Q)\,[J_0(Q\delta) - 1]\,Q\,\mathrm dQ = 2\int_\delta^\infty C(r)\,\frac{r\,\mathrm dr}{\sqrt{r^2-\delta^2}} - 2\int_0^\infty C(r)\,\mathrm dr , \]

in \([\text{length}]^{-2}\,\text{cm}^{-1}\) for \(I\) in cm⁻¹, as the .ses files give it (\(P/P_0 = e^{\lambda^2 t\,[G(\delta)-G(0)]}\)).

Forward model (projection_matrix()). For \(C\) piecewise linear on the r grid the Abel integrals are elementary, \(\int \frac{r\,\mathrm dr}{\sqrt{r^2-\delta^2}} = \sqrt{r^2-\delta^2}\) and \(\int \frac{r^2\,\mathrm dr}{\sqrt{r^2-\delta^2}} = \tfrac12\bigl[r\sqrt{r^2-\delta^2} + \delta^2\ln(r+\sqrt{r^2-\delta^2})\bigr]\), so \(G = A\,C\) is exact, square-root singularity included (DAB: \(2\delta K_1(\delta/\xi) - 2\xi\) to \(10^{-5}\); the Hankel transform of pySASstudio agrees to its own accuracy, \(3\cdot10^{-4}\)).

Inversion (invert_sesans()). \(C\) on \(r=0\) and 59 geometric nodes from \(\delta_{\min}/3\) to \(1.2\,\delta_{\max}\) (\(C = 0\) beyond) solves \(\min\,\lVert W(AC-G)\rVert^2 + \alpha\lVert DC\rVert^2\) (\(W = 1/\sigma_G\), \(D\) second differences, plus a pin of the last node), \(\alpha\) by the discrepancy principle (the largest \(\alpha\) with \(\chi^2 \le 1\); GCV without errors). \(\gamma_\text{SESANS}(r) = C(r)/C(0)\) is interpolated monotonically (PCHIP): kinks in \(\gamma\) would become kinks in \(g\) and spurious intensity at high \(q\).

Use in the reconstruction.

  • SESANS only: the \(\gamma\) of the SESANS curves enter the joint fit of §9 like scattering curves (their normalisation \(\lambda_m\) may lie between 0.05 and 20, as \(C(0)\) is extrapolated below \(\delta_{\min}\)).

  • With scattering curves: a pointwise compromise between a SESANS \(\gamma\) and a scattering \(\gamma\) is no transform pair of either, and the intensity at high \(q\) reacts strongly to that (tests: \(\chi^2 \sim 10^3\)). Instead, \(g_\text{SESANS}\) is found from the SESANS curves alone, set smoothly to 0 beyond the largest \(r\) at which their \(\gamma\) exceeds its uncertainty, and every scattering curve is extended below its \(q_{\min}\) by the level-cut model of \(g_\text{SESANS}\) for its own SLD set, scaled to its first points. This replaces the Guinier or constant extrapolation, which misses the invariant of structures larger than \(\pi/q_{\min}\). \(g(r)\) then follows from the extended scattering curves (exactly a transform pair of each), and the SESANS curves are a check: \(G_\text{model} = s\,A\,C_m(g)\) with one scale \(s\) fitted, \(\chi^2\) reported (absolute data: \(s\) against \(10^{-4}\) for Å, \(10^{-1}\) for nm).

  • Voxel structure: voxel_correlation() (autocorrelation of the SLD map, radial average up to \(L/2\)), projected with \(A\).

In the test (domains of 1500 Å, scattering data from \(q = 0.01\) Å⁻¹, SESANS from 50 to 8000 Å) the scattering curve alone misses the invariant by 40 % and \(g\) by 0.23; with the SESANS curve \(g\) is right to 0.04, \(\chi^2 = 0.07\) (scattering) and 1.9 (SESANS).

10. Surfaces

The 3D panel and the STL export draw iso-surfaces by marching cubes (Lorensen and Cline 1987; pysasfit.morph.surface). Three sources are possible:

  • Exact cut: the field \(\psi\) at the cut level, i.e. the smooth interface of the model. For a middle phase it is the pair of level surfaces.

  • Smoothed voxels: the voxel matrix smoothed with a Gaussian of 0.8 voxel.

  • Voxels as exported.

The volume is padded with one layer of the other phase, so the surface is closed at the box faces and at the clipping plane.

11. Box and grid

  • Box size: by default \(L = 5\cdot 2\pi/q^*\), with \(q^*\) the maximum of the Kratky plot \(q^2I\), but at most \(n\cdot 2\pi/q_\text{max}\) (suggest_box()).

  • Voxel size: the finest structure the data resolve is about \(\pi/q_\text{max}\). A warning is given when \(\pi/a < q_\text{max}\).

  • Correlations: \(\gamma(r)\) must have decayed within half the box diagonal (warning, §5).

12. Uniaxial anisotropy (texture)

pysasfit.morph.anisotropic; usage on pySASmorph: anisotropic data (texture).

The level cut holds direction by direction

Nothing in §4 needs isotropy. \(\psi(\mathbf 0)\) and \(\psi(\mathbf r)\) are bivariate normal with correlation \(g(\mathbf r)\) for every vector \(\mathbf r\), so

\[ S_2(\mathbf r)-\varphi^2 = F\bigl(g(\mathbf r)\bigr), \qquad g(\mathbf r) = F^{-1}\bigl(\varphi(1-\varphi)\,\gamma(\mathbf r)\bigr) . \]

The FFT synthesis of §6 works with any \(g(\mathbf r)\) sampled on the grid (field_from_grid_correlation()). What changes is how \(\gamma(\mathbf r)\) is obtained from the data.

Uniaxial symmetry: one 2D pattern is enough

Take a sample symmetric about an axis \(\hat{\mathbf e}\) in the detector plane, at the angle \(\psi\) from \(q_x\). Its intensity depends only on \(q\) and

\[ \mu = |\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}| , \]

since centrosymmetry folds \(\mu\) to \([0,1]\). At small angles the detector plane (\(q_z = 0\)) contains \(\hat{\mathbf e}\), so its points have every \(\mu\), and the 2D pattern determines \(I(q,\mu)\) completely. Along and across the axis:

\[ q_\parallel = q_x\cos\psi + q_y\sin\psi, \qquad q_\perp = -q_x\sin\psi + q_y\cos\psi . \]

Model B: Legendre components and the plane-wave expansion

Components. The intensity is expanded in even Legendre polynomials:

\[ I(q,\mu) = \sum_{l=0,2,\dots}^{l_\text{max}} I_l(q)\,P_l(\mu) . \]

The \(I_l\) are found by weighted least squares in q bins (legendre_components()). Two details matter:

  • Bin flattening: each pixel is first moved to the bin’s mean q along the azimuthal mean \(I_\text{ref}(q)\). Otherwise the correlation of q and μ inside a bin on a square pixel grid appears as a spurious anisotropy.

  • Coverage: a bin uses only as many orders as its μ coverage supports (occupied tenths of [0, 1], minus one). Bins without full coverage are left out.

Correlation components. With the plane-wave expansion \(e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r} = \sum_l (2l+1)\,i^l j_l(qr)\,P_l(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\) and the addition theorem, \(\int P_l(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e})\,P_{l'}(\hat{\mathbf q}\cdot\hat{\mathbf r})\, \mathrm d\Omega_q = \frac{4\pi}{2l+1}\delta_{ll'}P_l(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e})\):

\[ C(\mathbf r) = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^3}\int I(\mathbf q)\,e^{i\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf r}\,\mathrm d^3q = \sum_l C_l(r)\,P_l(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf e}), \qquad C_l(r) = \frac{(-1)^{l/2}}{2\pi^2}\int_0^\infty q^2 I_l(q)\,j_l(qr)\,\mathrm dq . \]

Then \(\gamma(\mathbf r) = C(\mathbf r)/C_0(0)\).

The isotropic part. Only \(l = 0\) contributes to the invariant:

\[ \int I\,\mathrm d^3q = 4\pi Q_0, \qquad Q_0 = \int q^2 I_0\,\mathrm dq = 2\pi^2\Delta\rho^2\varphi(1-\varphi), \]

so the contrast and \(S/V\) (from \(K_0\)) follow as in §1. \(C_0\) is the transform of §3, with the flat background B, the low-q extrapolation and the Porod tail all applied to \(I_0\).

Higher orders, \(l \ge 2\). \(I_l\) is extended in three ranges:

  • below the data as \(I_l(q_1)(q/q_1)^2\) (the anisotropy vanishes at \(q = 0\));

  • within the data, linearly;

  • beyond the data as \(K_l q^{-4}\), with \(K_l\) fitted on the Porod range.

The finite part is integrated on a fine uniform grid. The tail is analytic:

\[ \int_{q_N}^\infty q^2\,\frac{K_l}{q^4}\,j_l(qr)\,\mathrm dq = K_l\,r\int_{q_Nr}^\infty\frac{j_l(x)}{x^2}\,\mathrm dx, \qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{j_l(x)}{x^2}\,\mathrm dx = \frac{\sqrt\pi}{8}\,\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{l-1}{2}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\frac{l+4}{2}\bigr)} \quad (l\ge2), \]

e.g. π/16 for l = 2 (spherical_transform()).

Model A: affine stretch

An isotropic structure stretched by \(\lambda\) along the axis, \(\eta(\mathbf r) = \eta_\text{iso}(M^{-1}\mathbf r)\) with \(M = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda, 1, 1)\), has

\[ \gamma(\mathbf r) = \gamma_\text{iso}\Bigl(\sqrt{r_\parallel^2/\lambda^2 + r_\perp^2}\Bigr), \qquad I(\mathbf q) = \lambda\,I_\text{iso}\Bigl(\sqrt{\lambda^2 q_\parallel^2 + q_\perp^2}\Bigr) . \]

Fitting λ and the axis (fit_affine()). Both are fitted by collapsing \(\log I\) onto one curve of \(q_\text{eff}\). The misfit is the weighted scatter of \(\log I\) about its mean in quantile bins of \(q_\text{eff}\):

  • first a grid search over λ ∈ [0.2, 5];

  • then Nelder–Mead in (ln λ, ψ).

The collapsed curve \(I/\lambda\) against \(q_\text{eff}\) goes through the isotropic pipeline. Its invariant equals \(Q_0\), because \(\int I\,\mathrm d^3q\) is invariant under the stretch.

Which axis. In the plane, λ along ψ and 1/λ along ψ + 90° give the same ellipses. They differ in 3D, because the structure is rotationally symmetric about the chosen axis. The choice of axis therefore decides the 3D structure.

Field, model and checks

  • g on the grid. \(g(r,\mu)\) is tabulated on the r grid of §5 and 65 values of μ. The grid vectors take their value by bilinear interpolation. The axis is the box x axis.

  • Level-cut model. \(c(r,\mu) = F(g(r,\mu))\) is projected onto Legendre polynomials by 64-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature in μ. A uniform rule fails here, because \(c\) has a cusp at r = 0. Then

    \[ I^\text{lc}_l(q) = \Delta\rho^2\,4\pi\,(-1)^{l/2}\int_0^{r_\text{max}} r^2\,c_l(r)\,j_l(qr)\,\mathrm dr, \qquad c_l(r) = (2l+1)\int_0^1 c(r,\mu)\,P_l(\mu)\,\mathrm d\mu , \]

    up to \(l = 12\). χ² is taken over all pixels in the q range.

  • Voxel structure. The components come from all 3D wave vectors of \(|\mathrm{DFT}[\chi-\bar\chi]|^2\), with μ = |k_x|/k.

  • Texture measures (chord_lengths()):

    • the share of the interface faces normal to x, y and z (1/3 each when isotropic);

    • the mean chord length of each phase along each axis, \(\varphi\,n^3a/(N_d/2)\), where \(N_d\) is the number of faces normal to axis \(d\).
