pySCAtter: API reference

Generated from the docstrings.

Calculation back-end of the GUI: parameter dict (see params.py) -> hypscat objects -> 1D curve or 2D image.

Units: lengths in nm, q in 1/nm, detector distance and pixel sizes in m (only their ratio matters). Where the original C++ GUI semantics were not fully documented, the mapping chosen here is stated in the docstrings and in the README (“GUI” section).

pysasfit.scatter.engine.debye_waller(p, q)[source]

G(q) = exp(-sigma_DW^2 q^2 / 3): mean displacement sigma_DW (EditDebyeWaller) of the particles from their lattice points, isotropic in 3D (CrystalScatter: dwfactor = sigma^2/3; Foerster et al. 2005, eq. 25, with the absolute displacement). It damps the Bragg term and returns the intensity to the diffuse P(q) at high q.

pysasfit.scatter.engine.peak_delta(p, kind=None)[source]

Peak width delta (Foerster et al. 2011: Gaussian exp(-4x^2/(pi delta^2)), Lorentzian FWHM delta) and the paracrystal coefficient c (delta(q) = delta + c q^2), following CrystalScatter (sc_gpu_generic.h):

  • ‘Debye-Scherrer’ (RadButDebyeScherrer): delta = 4/D, D = EditDomainSize (eq. 92 of Foerster 2011);

  • ‘paracrystal’ (RadioButtonPara): delta = 4/D + (sigma_DW/dist)^2 dist q^2, dist = nearest-neighbour distance;

  • neither: delta = 1 (1/length), independent of D (as in CrystalScatter);

  • anisotropic Gaussian with isotropic orientation: delta = |(4/SigX, 4/SigY, 4/SigZ)| / 3.

pysasfit.scatter.engine.cpp_lattice_factor(p, c, qx, qy, qz, delta=None, para=None, powder=None)[source]

Z(q) of crystal c (reflections in the lab frame of the q vectors) in the CrystalScatter form above. delta / para: peak width and paracrystal coefficient (default: from peak_delta()).

Reflections of equal |G| share the radial factor and the normalisation; the angles to all reflections of a shell come from one matrix product. Gaussian factors are evaluated only where they exceed ~1e-20 of their maximum (exact to that level); Lorentzian-type factors everywhere (long tails).

powder: no azimuthal factor (every reflection spread uniformly over its sphere |q| = g: Debye-Scherrer rings, exact orientation average of this form). Default: True for Ordis = isotropic, where EditAzi does not apply.

pysasfit.scatter.engine.director_lab(p)[source]

Director (theta, phi) of the particle axis in the frame of the engine (x along the beam, y horizontal, z vertical). CrystalScatter defines it in its own frame (detector qx, qy; z along the beam): n_C = (sin theta cos phi, sin theta sin phi, cos theta) -> n = (n_Cz, n_Cx, n_Cy).

pysasfit.scatter.engine.intensity_1d(p, q)[source]

Isotropic I(q) at the given q (1/nm): powder-averaged lattice (LType) or the form factor, with the structure factor (no lattice), times I0 plus Base.

pysasfit.scatter.engine.smeared_1d(p, q, dq=None)[source]

pysasfit.scatter.engine.intensity_2d(p, qx, qy, qz, progress=None, should_abort=None)[source]

Intensity at arbitrary q vectors (arrays of one shape; beam along x): particle orientation distribution, oriented / fibre / powder lattice, structure factor (no lattice), times I0 plus Base.

Least-squares fit of pySCAtter model parameters to measured data (pySASred output or any 1D / 2D file the pyTOOLbox readers understand).

  • 1D: I(q) with Idev; the model is folded with the q resolution Qdev (Gaussian) when the file has it.

  • 2D: an NXcanSAS 2D image (Qx, Qy, I, Idev, mask); the model is evaluated at the q vector of every used pixel (beam along x, detector horizontal = model qy, vertical = model qz, the beam component from the Ewald sphere when the wavelength is known). Pixels can be thinned (every n-th) to keep a fit fast.

Fitted parameters are the numeric parameters with a fit flag (the SasScatter FITFLAG_), bounded by their ranges in params. Lengths in nm, q in 1/nm as everywhere in pySCAtter.

pysasfit.scatter.params.relevant(p)[source]

Numeric parameters that the calculation uses with the combo settings of p.

Models that the hypergeometric (hypscat) part of pySCAtter does not cover, added for the pySCAtter tool:

  • KratkyPorodChain – worm-like chain, Kholodenko’s approximation (Macromolecules 26, 4179 (1993)), Schulz-Zimm polydisperse in the contour length.

  • powerlaw_shell_sphere() – core + shell with an algebraic segment-density profile φ(r) ∝ r^α (Förster & Burger, Macromolecules 31, 879 (1998): star polymers / block-copolymer micelles), as a stack of thin homogeneous shells of the exact multi-shell sphere.

  • TeubnerStrey – microemulsion / bicontinuous model (Teubner & Strey, J. Chem. Phys. 87, 3195 (1987)).

  • Isotropic structure factors for the decoupling approximation I = P (1 + β (S - 1)): PercusYevick (hard spheres), StickyHardSphere (Baxter / Menon et al.), FractalTeixeira (mass fractal of building blocks, Teixeira J. Appl. Cryst. 21, 781 (1988)).

All lengths in the same unit (nm in the GUI), q in the inverse unit. P(0) = 1 for the form factors.

pysasfit.scatter.models_ext.make_structure_factor(p)[source]

Structure factor of the parameter dict (key StructureFactor), or None.

Particle shapes: orientation-averaged and orientation-resolved Schulz-Zimm averaged form factors. All results are normalised so that P(q->0) = 1 and F(q->0) = 1.

Conventions

R mean size (scale of all dimensions; aspect ratios are fixed), sigma relative width of the Schulz-Zimm size distribution (0 = monodisperse), volume weighting: P ~ V^2 -> s = 6, F ~ V -> s = 3 (all bodies are 3D).
